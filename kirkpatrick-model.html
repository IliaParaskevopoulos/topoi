Sales enablement

Kirkpatrick Model

Measure sales training at four levels: reaction, learning, behaviour and business results.

Donald Kirkpatrick, 1959

  1. Did sellers find the training useful and relevant?

    Sellers rate the new pricing training 4.4 out of 5.

  2. Did they learn what was intended? Test it.

    92% pass a short quiz on the new plans.

  3. Do they apply it in real work? Look at calls, CRM data and coaching.

    70% of calls now mention the CHF 49 plan.

  4. Did it change business results?

    Share of new customers on the CHF 49 plan rises from 20% to 34%.

The question it answers

Did our enablement actually change what sellers do, and did it pay off?

When to use it

  • Planning a sales training or enablement programme.
  • Showing leadership the value of enablement.
  • Deciding which programmes to keep.

When to skip it

  • Tiny one-off briefings, where measuring all four levels costs more than it tells you.

How to run it

  1. Start from the result you want, then design backwards.
  2. Decide how to measure each level before training starts.
  3. Measure behaviour through call reviews and CRM data.
  4. Report results three months after training.

Worked example: Kontor

Kontor is a fictional fintech that lets small businesses get paid early on their invoices.

Common mistakes

  • Stopping at satisfaction surveys.
  • No baseline, so results cannot be compared.
  • Claiming results that other changes caused.
