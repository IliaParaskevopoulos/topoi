Pricing and packaging

Price Waterfall

Follow the price from list to what you actually keep, to find where money leaks away.

Michael Marn and Robert Rosiello, McKinsey, 1992

  1. Start with the official price.

    EUR 120 per tonne.

  2. Subtract discounts given on the invoice.

    Volume discount: invoice price EUR 104.

  3. Subtract everything paid after the invoice: rebates, freight, payment terms, promotions.

    Freight, year-end rebates and 90-day terms: pocket price EUR 88.

  4. Compare pocket prices across customers. Large differences often have no good reason.

    Two similar customers differ by EUR 14 per tonne, mostly freight. Halden changes delivery terms.

The question it answers

How much of our list price do we actually keep, and where does the rest go?

When to use it

  • Margins fall even though list prices hold.
  • Sales teams have wide freedom on discounts.
  • Before a pricing or discount policy review.

When to skip it

  • When you lack transaction-level data. Collect it first.

How to run it

  1. Gather every price element for a sample of transactions.
  2. Build the waterfall for each customer.
  3. Compare pocket prices for similar customers.
  4. Fix the biggest unexplained leaks with clear policies.

Common mistakes

  • Looking only at invoice discounts and missing off-invoice costs.
  • Treating all discounts as bad; some buy volume or loyalty.
  • Policies sales cannot follow.
