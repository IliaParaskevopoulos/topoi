Brand

95-5 Rule

At any time, only about 5% of B2B buyers are ready to buy. Marketing must also reach the 95% who will buy later.

John Dawes, Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, for the LinkedIn B2B Institute, 2021

  1. Most buyers in your category are not looking right now. Reach them so they remember you when they are.

    Most plants are happy with their tools this quarter.

  2. A small share is actively buying in a given quarter. Lead generation only reaches these.

    If plants change maintenance software about every five years, roughly 5% are in market each quarter.

  3. Build memory with the 95% and convert the 5%. Judge brand work by future demand, not this quarter’s leads.

    Tessera keeps a steady downtime-insight programme for all plants, plus campaigns for those in market.

The question it answers

Are we only chasing buyers who are ready now, and ignoring the ones who will buy later?

When to use it

  • Making the case for brand building in B2B.
  • Lead targets that ignore long buying cycles.
  • Explaining why performance marketing alone stalls.

When to skip it

  • Categories bought very frequently, where most buyers are in market often.

How to run it

  1. Estimate how often a customer buys or switches in your category.
  2. Calculate the share in market per quarter.
  3. Plan reach and memory-building for the rest.
  4. Measure brand awareness and consideration, not just leads.

Worked example: Tessera

Tessera is a fictional SaaS company whose software helps mid-size manufacturers plan and track equipment maintenance.

Common mistakes

  • Judging all marketing by this quarter’s leads.
  • Hard-selling to people who are not ready.
  • Treating 5% as a fixed number; it depends on your purchase cycle.
