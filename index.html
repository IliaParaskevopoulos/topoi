Customer understanding

Who buys, what they are trying to get done, and what stands in their way.

Jobs-to-be-Done

Clayton Christensen and colleagues, 2003; job stories from Intercom, around 2013

Customers do not buy products; they “hire” them to make progress in a specific situation.

Value Proposition Canvas

Alexander Osterwalder and colleagues, Strategyzer, 2014

Two shapes side by side: what a customer is trying to get done, and how your product helps. Fit is where they match.

Kano Model

Noriaki Kano and colleagues, 1984

Not every feature affects satisfaction the same way. Some are expected, some scale with effort, and some delight.

Market and competitive strategy

Where to play, and how strong your position is there.

SWOT and TOWS

SWOT: 1960s, origin disputed. TOWS: Heinz Weihrich, 1982

Strengths and weaknesses inside the company, opportunities and threats outside it, turned into actions.

Porter’s Five Forces

Michael Porter, 1979

Five pressures that decide how much profit an industry leaves for the companies in it.

Ansoff Matrix

Igor Ansoff, 1957

Four ways to grow, sorted by how far each one takes you from what you already sell and who you already sell to.

BCG Growth-Share Matrix

Bruce Henderson, Boston Consulting Group, 1970

Sort products by market growth and market share, to decide where to invest and where to take cash out.

TAM, SAM, SOM

Common market-sizing practice; no single author

Three nested market sizes: everyone who could ever buy, those you can actually serve, and the share you can realistically win.

Positioning

The context that makes your product’s value obvious to the right buyer.

Obviously Awesome

April Dunford, 2019

A positioning method that starts from what customers would do without you, then works out what makes you different and who cares most.

Positioning Statement

Geoffrey Moore, 1991

One structured sentence that says who the product is for, what it is, why it matters and how it differs.

Whole Product

Theodore Levitt, 1980; adapted by Geoffrey Moore, 1991

Customers buy more than the core product: everything around it that makes it work for them.

Crossing the Chasm

Geoffrey Moore, 1991, building on Everett Rogers

New technology spreads through groups of buyers in order, and the hardest jump is from early enthusiasts to the pragmatic mainstream.

Messaging and storytelling

Turning positioning into words that sales, web and campaigns can use.

Messaging House

Practitioner tool, widely used in B2B marketing and PR

One umbrella message, held up by three pillars with proof, standing on what makes you credible.

Golden Circle

Simon Sinek, 2009

Explain why you exist before how you work and what you sell.

Features, Advantages, Benefits

Classic sales-training technique; no single author

Turn what a product has into what it does, and then into what the customer gains.

Brand

Brand strength is built in four steps, from being known at all to customers feeling a real bond with you.

Pricing and packaging

What to charge, how to package it, and what buyers will accept.

Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter

Peter van Westendorp, 1976

Four questions about price that show the range buyers find acceptable, and where in that range to aim.

Good, Better, Best

Long-standing retail practice; now standard in software pricing

Three versions at three prices, so each segment finds its fit and most choose the middle.

Go-to-market and launch

Getting a product to market: motions, plans and launch mechanics.

Launch Tiers

Common product marketing practice; no single author

Not every release deserves a big launch. Sort releases into tiers, each with its own level of effort.

Product Lifecycle

Joel Dean, 1950; popularised by Theodore Levitt, 1965

Products move through introduction, growth, maturity and decline, and each stage calls for different marketing.

Sales enablement

SPIN Selling

Neil Rackham, 1988

Four kinds of question that move a buyer from describing their situation to stating the value of a solution.

Growth and lifecycle

How customers move from first contact to loyal advocate, and how to measure it.

AARRR Pirate Metrics

Dave McClure, 2007

Five stages that turn the customer journey into numbers, so you can see where you lose people.

Flywheel

HubSpot, 2018, building on Jim Collins’s flywheel idea

A circle instead of a funnel: happy customers feed growth, so energy you put in keeps the wheel turning.

See, Think, Do, Care

Avinash Kaushik, 2013

Plan content and measurement by how much buying intent each audience has, from none yet to loyal customer.

Hook Model

Nir Eyal, 2014

Four steps that, repeated, turn using a product into a habit: trigger, action, variable reward, investment.

Content and channels

Planning content and choosing where it runs.

Hub, Hero, Hygiene

YouTube creator guidance, around 2014

Three layers of content: always-on answers, regular series, and a few big moments.

PESO Model

Gini Dietrich, 2014

Four kinds of media, paid, earned, shared and owned, that work best when one piece of work feeds all of them.

Planning and prioritization

RICE Scoring

Sean McBride, Intercom, around 2016

Score ideas on reach, impact, confidence and effort, so you can compare them fairly.

Opportunity Solution Tree

Teresa Torres, Product Talk

A map from one business outcome to the customer needs behind it, the ideas that could meet them, and the tests that check those ideas.
