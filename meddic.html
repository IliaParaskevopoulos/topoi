Sales enablement

MEDDIC

Six things you must know to qualify a complex B2B deal, and to tell a real opportunity from hope.

Jack Napoli and Dick Dunkel, PTC, 1996

  1. The measurable gain the customer expects, in their own numbers: money saved, revenue gained, risk reduced.

    Cut rejected glass batches from 3% to 1%, worth about EUR 2 million a year.

  2. The person who can approve the spend, and say yes when others say no. Have you met them?

    The plant’s operations director, who signs supply contracts above EUR 5 million.

  3. The formal and informal criteria the customer will use to compare options.

    Iron content below 100 ppm, delivery reliability, and price per tonne delivered.

  4. The steps, people and timeline from evaluation to signature.

    Lab qualification, a three-month trial, a procurement tender, then board sign-off.

  5. The business pain that makes the purchase necessary, and what happens if nothing changes.

    Off-spec sand from the current supplier caused two line stoppages last year.

  6. Someone inside the customer with influence who wants you to win and sells for you when you are not in the room.

    The quality manager, who led the investigation into the stoppages.

  7. Mark each letter as confirmed, partly known or a gap. Gaps become the next actions. A deal with several gaps is not ready to forecast.

    Halden has not met the operations director yet, so getting that meeting through the champion is the next step.

The question it answers

Do we know enough about this deal to forecast it, and what do we need to find out next?

When to use it

  • Complex B2B deals with several stakeholders and a long sales cycle.
  • Deal and forecast reviews, to replace gut feeling with evidence.
  • Coaching sellers on what to find out in discovery.

When to skip it

  • Fast, low-value transactional sales, where it adds work without adding insight.
  • Early lead qualification by marketing. Use a lighter check there.

How to run it

  1. Agree what “confirmed” means for each letter, so scores are comparable across sellers.
  2. After each discovery call, update the letters with evidence, not opinion.
  3. In deal reviews, go letter by letter and turn each gap into a dated next action.
  4. Product marketing can help by writing discovery questions and proof for each letter, per segment.
  5. Add Competition (MEDDICC) or Paper process (MEDDPICC) if those often derail your deals.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a friendly contact as a champion. A champion has influence and acts for you.
  • Writing metrics in your terms instead of the customer’s.
  • Filling the checklist once and never updating it.
