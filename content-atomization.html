Content and channels

Content Atomization

Turn one substantial piece of content into many smaller pieces for different channels.

Todd Defren, 2008

  1. Choose one substantial piece: a report, webinar, study or guide.

    Tessera’s webinar on cutting unplanned downtime.

  2. Cut it into smaller pieces, each useful on its own and fitted to a channel.

    Three short clips, a blog post, a checklist, five social posts, a newsletter piece.

  3. Every small piece points back to the full piece.

    Each clip links to the full webinar recording.

The question it answers

Are we getting full value from each big piece of content we create?

When to use it

  • After producing a major report, webinar or study.
  • Small teams that need more reach without more production.
  • Planning a launch content calendar.

When to skip it

  • Thin content that does not hold up when split.

How to run it

  1. Plan the pieces before you create the big piece.
  2. Cut pieces that stand alone and suit each channel.
  3. Schedule them over weeks, not one day.
  4. Link every piece back to the source.

Worked example: Tessera

Common mistakes

  • Posting the same text in every channel.
  • Pieces that make no sense without the original.
  • Releasing everything at once.
