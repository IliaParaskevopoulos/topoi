Market and competitive analysis

BCG Growth-Share Matrix

Sort products by market growth and market share, to decide where to invest and where to take cash out.

Bruce Henderson, Boston Consulting Group, 1970

The question it answers

Which products should we invest in, which should fund the others, and which should we let go?

When to use it

  • Reviewing a portfolio of products, brands or business units.
  • Explaining to leadership why marketing budget is uneven across products.
  • Deciding which products to phase out.

When to skip it

  • Single-product companies.
  • Markets where share does not bring cost or scale advantages. The logic depends on them.

How to run it

  1. Define each product’s market precisely, then find its growth rate.
  2. Calculate relative share: your share divided by the largest competitor’s.
  3. Place each product, sized by revenue if you can.
  4. Decide a role per product: invest, hold, harvest or exit.
  5. Check the portfolio has enough question marks and stars for the future.

Common mistakes

  • Calling every product a star.
  • Defining markets narrowly so that every product leads its own niche.
  • Starving a dog that customers need to buy the rest of the range.
