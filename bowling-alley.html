Bowling Alley

After the first segment, expand to neighbouring segments one by one, like knocking down bowling pins.

Geoffrey Moore, Inside the Tornado, 1995

  1. The first segment you win, which knocks over the others.

    Paediatric practices.

  2. Segments next to the head pin: same use case with new customers, or same customers with a new use case.

    General practices (same use case) and urgent-care centres (similar need).

  3. Segments reached through the second row’s references and partners.

    Pharmacies, care homes and occupational health services.

  4. Pick the next segment where your references and whole product carry over best.

    Lumora picks general practices: the same reimbursement code and the same associations.

The question it answers

Which segment should we move into next, using what we won in the last?

When to use it

  • You have won a first segment and need to grow.
  • Choosing between several expansion options.
  • Explaining the growth path to investors or leadership.

When to skip it

  • Before you have truly won the first segment.

How to run it

  1. List segments adjacent to your current one.
  2. For each, note what carries over: references, product, channels.
  3. Order them by how much carries over and how big they are.
  4. Move one pin at a time.

Worked example: Lumora Diagnostics

Lumora Diagnostics is a fictional company that makes a rapid blood-test reader for GP clinics.

Common mistakes

  • Jumping to a big but unrelated segment.
  • Expanding before the head pin is secure.
  • Ignoring what new segments need in the whole product.
