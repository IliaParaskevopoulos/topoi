Messaging and storytelling

Messaging House

One umbrella message, held up by three pillars with proof, standing on what makes you credible.

Practitioner tool, widely used in B2B marketing and PR

  1. The roof: the one thing you want your audience to believe about you. One sentence, in the customer’s terms.

    Get paid today for invoices your customers pay in 60 days.

  2. Each pillar is one reason to believe the umbrella message. Start with the reason that matters most to the buyer.

    Speed: cash in your account within 24 hours.

  3. A second, distinct reason. Pillars should not overlap.

    Simplicity: connect your accounting software and pick an invoice. No paperwork.

  4. A third reason. Three is usual: enough to be convincing, few enough to remember.

    Clear cost: one fixed fee per invoice, shown before you submit.

  5. Under each pillar, the evidence: numbers, customer examples, certifications, demos. A pillar without proof is a claim.

    Median payout time of six hours. Setup in ten minutes. Fee calculator on every invoice.

  6. The base: who you are and why you are credible. Company facts that support every message above.

    Supervised financial intermediary, 2,000 small-business customers, backed by a cantonal bank partner.

The question it answers

What do we want our audience to believe, and why should they believe it?

When to use it

  • After positioning is agreed and before writing the website, sales deck or campaign.
  • Teams describe the product in different ways and you need one version.
  • Briefing agencies, new hires or partners.

When to skip it

  • Before positioning is settled. The house will just record the confusion.
  • As customer-facing copy. It is an internal tool; the words still need adapting to each channel.

How to run it

  1. Start from your positioning: best-fit customer, value and proof.
  2. Write the umbrella message in the customer’s language, and test it on a few customers.
  3. Choose three pillars that are distinct and each matter to the buyer.
  4. Collect proof for each pillar. Drop or rework any pillar you cannot prove.
  5. Share it, then check a month later that the website, deck and campaigns use it.

Kontor is a fictional fintech that lets small businesses get paid early on their invoices.

Common mistakes

  • An umbrella message that could belong to any competitor.
  • Pillars that describe features rather than reasons to believe.
  • Making a separate house per persona too early. Start with one, then adapt the proof by audience.
