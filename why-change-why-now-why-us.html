Messaging and storytelling

Why Change, Why Now, Why Us

Most deals are lost to “no decision”. Answer why the buyer should change, why now, and why you.

Erik Peterson and Tim Riesterer, Corporate Visions, 2011

  1. Show what is wrong with the current way, in the buyer’s terms. The status quo is your biggest competitor.

    Fixed maintenance schedules miss failures and waste technician time.

  2. Give a reason to act soon: a cost of waiting, a deadline or a shift.

    Skilled technicians are scarce, and insurers now ask for maintenance records.

  3. Only now explain why you are the best way to change.

    Live in a day, with no IT project and early failure warnings.

  4. Show that the value clearly exceeds the cost.

    Two avoided breakdowns pay for a year of Tessera.

The question it answers

Have we given buyers a reason to change at all, before asking them to choose us?

When to use it

  • Many deals end in “no decision”.
  • Sales pitches jump straight to features.
  • Early-stage categories where buyers are not yet looking.

When to skip it

  • Late-stage deals where the buyer has already decided to change and is comparing vendors.

How to run it

  1. List what the status quo costs the buyer.
  2. Find a credible reason to act now.
  3. Write why you are best only after the first two answers are strong.
  4. Build a simple value calculation for why pay.

Worked example: Tessera

Tessera is a fictional SaaS company whose software helps mid-size manufacturers plan and track equipment maintenance.

Why change
Fixed maintenance schedules miss failures and waste technician time.
Why now
Skilled technicians are scarce, and insurers now ask for maintenance records.
Why us
Live in a day, with no IT project and early failure warnings.
Why pay
Two avoided breakdowns pay for a year of Tessera.

Common mistakes

  • Starting with why us.
  • Urgency that is invented rather than real.
  • Ignoring the risk buyers feel in changing.
