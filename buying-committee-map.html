Buying Committee Map

Map the people behind a B2B decision, what each cares about, and where each stands.

Common B2B sales and marketing practice

  1. Put the decision in the middle. B2B purchases usually involve a group, often six or more people.

    A five-year supply contract for solar-grade sand.

  2. The person who approves the spend and owns the business result.

    The plant’s operations director.

  3. The person who wants you to win and will sell for you internally.

    The quality manager who investigated last year’s line stoppages.

  4. The person who checks whether you meet the requirements.

    The process engineer who runs the lab qualification.

  5. People affected by the decision: users, procurement, finance, legal.

    Furnace operators, and procurement, who run the tender.

  6. Note whether each person supports, is neutral, or is sceptical, and plan who needs what.

    Procurement is sceptical about price, so Halden prepares a cost-per-good-tonne comparison.

The question it answers

Who decides, who influences, and what does each of them need to hear?

When to use it

  • Planning a complex deal or key account.
  • Writing messages and content for each role.
  • Spotting a deal that depends on one contact.

When to skip it

  • Simple purchases made by one person.

How to run it

  1. List everyone involved, from discovery calls and CRM notes.
  2. Assign each a role and note what they care about.
  3. Mark their stance and how well you know them.
  4. Plan the message, proof and contact each one needs.
  5. Update after every important meeting.

Common mistakes

  • Mapping titles instead of real roles.
  • Assuming a friendly contact is a champion.
  • Leaving out procurement and finance until the end.
