Messaging and storytelling

Golden Circle

Explain why you exist before how you work and what you sell.

Simon Sinek, 2009

  1. The centre: the purpose or belief behind the company. Not “to make money”, which is a result.

    We believe a weekday dinner should be real food, even when there’s no time to cook.

  2. How you act on that belief: the principles, methods or choices that make you different.

    Small-batch recipes, Swiss ingredients, and nothing you wouldn’t use at home.

  3. The outside: the products and services you sell. Everyone knows their what.

    Chilled ready meals in Swiss supermarkets.

  4. Sinek’s argument is that people respond more to the why than to the what, so lead with it.

    Orto’s packaging opens with the belief about weekday dinners, then the recipe principles, then the meal.

The question it answers

Can we explain why we do what we do, not only what we sell?

When to use it

  • Opening a company story, keynote or About page.
  • Aligning founders and leaders on purpose.
  • Checking that brand messaging says more than features.

When to skip it

  • Product pages where buyers need specifics first.
  • When the “why” is invented for marketing and the company does not act on it.

How to run it

  1. Ask founders and long-time staff why the company started and what they believe.
  2. Write the why as a belief, in one sentence.
  3. List two or three hows that prove the belief in practice.
  4. Write the what plainly.
  5. Test the story inside out with customers and staff.

Worked example: Orto Foods

Orto Foods is a fictional maker of chilled ready meals sold through Swiss supermarkets.

Why
We believe a weekday dinner should be real food, even when there’s no time to cook.
How
Small-batch recipes, Swiss ingredients, and nothing you wouldn’t use at home.
What
Chilled ready meals in Swiss supermarkets.
Communicate from the inside out
Orto’s packaging opens with the belief about weekday dinners, then the recipe principles, then the meal.

Common mistakes

  • A why that any competitor could claim.
  • A why with no proof in how the company behaves.
  • Relying on the talk’s claims about brain biology, which are not well supported. The communication advice stands on its own.
