Sales enablement

Command of the Message

A value framework that ties buyers’ problems to business outcomes, the capabilities needed, and your proof.

Force Management, 2000s

  1. How the buyer works today, and what is wrong with it.

    Maintenance runs on spreadsheets and fixed schedules.

  2. What the current way costs.

    Unplanned breakdowns, overtime and audit findings.

  3. How things look once the problem is solved.

    Failures are spotted early and work is planned.

  4. The business results the buyer will see.

    Higher output and lower maintenance costs.

  5. What any solution must be able to do, written so your strengths stand out.

    Warn before failure, work offline, go live without IT.

  6. How the buyer will measure success.

    Unplanned downtime hours, emergency repair costs.

  7. Evidence you have delivered these outcomes before.

    18% less downtime at a pilot plant; live in a day.

The question it answers

Can every seller connect our product to the outcomes buyers care about?

When to use it

  • Sellers pitch features instead of outcomes.
  • Building discovery guides and value messaging together with sales.
  • Onboarding new sellers.

When to skip it

  • Small transactional sales where buyers do not need a business case.

How to run it

  1. Interview customers about before, after, outcomes and metrics.
  2. Write required capabilities that favour your strengths.
  3. Attach proof to each outcome.
  4. Turn the framework into discovery questions and train sellers.

Worked example: Tessera

Tessera is a fictional SaaS company whose software helps mid-size manufacturers plan and track equipment maintenance.

Common mistakes

  • A framework written by marketing alone.
  • Capabilities that any competitor can claim.
  • No metrics, so buyers cannot build a business case.
