Sales enablement

Value Selling

A simple formula: vision times value times power equals a plan to a decision.

ValueSelling Associates, 1990s

  1. The buyer’s own picture of how their problem is solved, ideally including your capabilities.

    The owner pictures paying staff on time even when customers pay late.

  2. The measurable value the buyer expects, in their terms.

    No more overdraft fees, and no late supplier payments.

  3. Access to the people who can approve and fund the change.

    The owner and the external accountant.

  4. An agreed plan to a decision. If any of the three is zero, there is no deal.

    Connect the software this week; finance the first invoice next week.

The question it answers

Does the buyer have a vision, see the value, and can they make it happen?

When to use it

  • Deal reviews, to see what is missing.
  • Training sellers to sell on value.
  • Mid-size B2B deals with a few decision-makers.

When to skip it

  • Very large deals with complex buying groups; combine it with MEDDIC.

How to run it

  1. For each deal, write the buyer’s vision in their words.
  2. Agree the value with the buyer, in numbers if possible.
  3. Confirm who has power to decide.
  4. Agree a written plan to a decision.

Worked example: Kontor

Kontor is a fictional fintech that lets small businesses get paid early on their invoices.

Common mistakes

  • A vision written by the seller, not the buyer.
  • Value that the buyer never confirmed.
  • Moving forward without access to power.
