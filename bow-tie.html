Adoption and growth

Bow-Tie Model

A funnel that does not stop at the sale: after the commitment, the customer journey widens again through onboarding, impact and growth.

Winning by Design (Jacco van der Kooij), 2010s

  1. Buyers become aware they have a problem and that you exist.

    12,000 plant managers reached by downtime content.

  2. Buyers learn how to solve the problem and what good looks like.

    600 attend webinars or download the downtime guide.

  3. Buyers compare options and choose.

    90 plants run a demo or pilot.

  4. The knot of the bow tie: the customer signs.

    30 plants sign.

  5. The customer gets set up and reaches first value.

    28 plants live within two weeks.

  6. The customer sees the results they bought it for. This drives renewal.

    Average 15% less unplanned downtime after six months.

  7. The customer renews, expands and recommends. In recurring revenue, this side is where most lifetime value comes from.

    A third add the Predict module or a second plant in year two.

The question it answers

Are we investing in the half of the journey where recurring revenue is made?

When to use it

  • Subscription and recurring-revenue businesses.
  • Marketing that stops at the sale.
  • Aligning marketing, sales and customer success on one model.

When to skip it

  • One-off purchases with no ongoing relationship.

How to run it

  1. Define each stage and its conversion metric.
  2. Measure conversion on both sides of the commit.
  3. Find the weakest conversion and fix it first.
  4. Give product marketing a role on the right side: onboarding content, adoption campaigns, expansion offers.

Worked example: Tessera

Tessera is a fictional SaaS company whose software helps mid-size manufacturers plan and track equipment maintenance.

Common mistakes

  • Celebrating new logos while churn rises.
  • No clear owner for onboarding and impact.
  • Measuring impact by usage instead of customer outcomes.
