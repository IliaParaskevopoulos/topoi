Net Promoter Score

One question about how likely customers are to recommend you, turned into a score you can track.

Fred Reichheld, Bain and Company, 2003

  1. “How likely are you to recommend us to a friend or colleague?” on a scale from 0 to 10. The line shows how answers are spread.

    Nordhaus asks every customer a week after delivery.

  2. Scores 0 to 6: unhappy customers who may discourage others.

    18% of answers.

  3. Scores 7 and 8: satisfied but not enthusiastic.

    30% of answers.

  4. Scores 9 and 10: loyal customers who recommend you.

    52% of answers.

  5. NPS = % promoters minus % detractors, from −100 to +100. Always ask “why?” as a follow-up.

    52 − 18 = 34. Detractors mostly mention late deliveries.

The question it answers

How loyal are our customers, and why do some recommend us and others not?

When to use it

  • Tracking customer loyalty over time.
  • Finding which experiences create promoters and detractors.
  • Choosing customers for references and case studies.

When to skip it

  • As a target for teams to game.
  • As the only measure; pair it with retention and satisfaction data such as CSAT and Customer Effort Score.

How to run it

  1. Ask at consistent moments, such as after delivery or onboarding.
  2. Always add an open “why?” question.
  3. Track the score over time and by segment, not as one number.
  4. Read the comments monthly and act on the top themes.
  5. Follow up with detractors personally.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing scores across industries, where norms differ.
  • Ignoring the reasons behind the number.
  • Surveying so often that customers stop answering.
