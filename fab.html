Features, Advantages, Benefits

Turn what a product has into what it does, and then into what the customer gains.

Classic sales-training technique; no single author

  1. What the product is or has: a fact, a specification, a component.

    Optical sorting and acid washing at the quarry.

  2. What that feature does, or how it performs better than the alternative.

    Iron content below 100 ppm in every shipment.

  3. What the customer gains, in their terms: money, time, risk, status.

    Fewer rejected solar-glass batches and higher line output.

  4. Keep asking “so what?” until you reach something the buyer measures or reports on.

    Higher output means meeting delivery targets without adding furnace capacity.

The question it answers

Are we describing what the product has, or what the customer gets from it?

When to use it

  • Writing product pages, brochures and sales sheets.
  • Training new sellers to talk about outcomes.
  • Reviewing copy that reads like a specification sheet.

When to skip it

  • Highly technical buyers who first need the specification. Give it to them, then the benefit.
  • Positioning work. FAB works at message level, after positioning is set.

How to run it

  1. List the features that matter for one buyer role.
  2. For each, write what it does better than the alternative.
  3. Translate that into a benefit in the buyer’s own measures.
  4. Ask “so what?” until the benefit is something they report on.
  5. Lead copy with the benefit, and keep the feature as proof.

Worked example: Halden Minerals

Halden Minerals is a fictional supplier of high-purity silica sand to glass and solar-panel makers.

Feature
Optical sorting and acid washing at the quarry.
Advantage
Iron content below 100 ppm in every shipment.
Benefit
Fewer rejected solar-glass batches and higher line output.
Ask “so what?”
Higher output means meeting delivery targets without adding furnace capacity.

Common mistakes

  • Benefits so general they fit any product (“saves time”).
  • Using the same benefit for every buyer role.
  • Dropping the feature entirely, which removes the proof.
