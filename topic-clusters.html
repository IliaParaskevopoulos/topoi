Topic Clusters

Organise content around one broad pillar page linked to detailed pages on related subtopics.

HubSpot, 2017

  1. One broad, thorough page on a core topic your buyers search for.

    “The complete guide to getting paid faster.”

  2. Detailed pages on specific subtopics, each answering one question in depth.

    Invoice financing explained; payment terms; cash-flow forecasting; chasing late payments.

  3. Link every cluster page to the pillar and back, so readers and search engines see the topic as a whole.

    Each cluster page links to the guide; the guide links to all four.

The question it answers

Is our content organised so search engines and readers see us as the authority on our topics?

When to use it

  • Planning content for search.
  • A blog full of unconnected posts.
  • Building authority on a few core topics.

When to skip it

  • Content that is not meant to be found through search, such as sales enablement.

How to run it

  1. Pick three to five core topics your buyers search for.
  2. Research the questions under each topic.
  3. Write one pillar page and several cluster pages per topic.
  4. Link them, and update the pillar as clusters are added.

Worked example: Kontor

Kontor is a fictional fintech that lets small businesses get paid early on their invoices.

Pillar page
“The complete guide to getting paid faster.”
Cluster pages
Invoice financing explained; payment terms; cash-flow forecasting; chasing late payments.
Link them
Each cluster page links to the guide; the guide links to all four.

Common mistakes

  • Pillars that are too thin to be useful.
  • Clusters that repeat each other.
  • Forgetting internal links.
