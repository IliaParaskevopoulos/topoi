Go-to-market and launch

Launch Tiers

Not every release deserves a big launch. Sort releases into tiers, each with its own level of effort.

Common product marketing practice; no single author

  1. Small changes and fixes. Tell the people who need to know, with little extra effort.

    A new report template: release note, in-app message and help-centre update.

  2. Meaningful improvements for existing customers. Some marketing, some sales enablement.

    An SAP connector: customer webinar, sales training and partner announcement.

  3. Major launches that change the market position or open new segments. Full campaign and full enablement.

    Tessera Predict: launch event, press, campaign and a free pilot programme.

  4. Choose the tier with agreed criteria such as revenue impact, customers affected and competitive importance, not by who asks loudest.

    Tessera scores each release on revenue, customers affected and differentiation, from 1 to 3 each.

The question it answers

How much launch effort does this release deserve?

When to use it

  • Product ships often and marketing cannot launch everything.
  • Product and marketing disagree about how big a launch should be.
  • Planning the launch calendar for the year.

When to skip it

  • A company with one or two releases a year. Plan each one individually.

How to run it

  1. Agree three to five criteria and a simple score for each.
  2. Define a fixed list of activities for each tier.
  3. Score each release early, together with product.
  4. Assign owners and dates from the tier’s list.
  5. Review after launch and adjust the tier definitions.

Worked example: Tessera

Tessera is a fictional SaaS company whose software helps mid-size manufacturers plan and track equipment maintenance.

Common mistakes

  • Everything becoming Tier 1.
  • Deciding the tier too late to prepare.
  • Skipping Tier 3 communication entirely, which leaves customers unaware of improvements.
