Pricing and packaging

Leaders, Fillers, Killers

Sort features into those that drive the purchase, those that fill out a package, and those that put buyers off.

Madhavan Ramanujam and Georg Tacke, Simon-Kucher, 2016

  1. Features that most buyers value highly and would pay for. Build each package around one or two.

    Fast payout and one fixed fee.

  2. Nice-to-have features that add value but do not drive the decision. Use them to round out packages.

    Payment reminders and cash-flow reports.

  3. Features or terms that put buyers off, even if a few want them. Leave them out or make them optional.

    A mandatory annual contract, or having to finance every invoice.

  4. Each package needs its own leader, so buyers know why to choose it.

    Basic leads with next-day payout; Business leads with same-day payout.

The question it answers

Which features should define our packages, and which should we leave out?

When to use it

  • Creating or reworking packages.
  • Packages that are crowded with features nobody pays for.
  • Deciding what to charge extra for.

When to skip it

  • Products with one simple offer and no packaging decisions.

How to run it

  1. List all features and terms.
  2. Research willingness to pay and appeal for each, by segment.
  3. Sort them into leaders, fillers and killers.
  4. Build packages around leaders, add fillers, remove killers.

Worked example: Kontor

Kontor is a fictional fintech that lets small businesses get paid early on their invoices.

Common mistakes

  • Bundling a killer into every package.
  • Packages with no clear leader.
  • Sorting features by internal opinion rather than research.
