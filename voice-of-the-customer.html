Customer understanding

Voice of the Customer

A steady programme that collects what customers say, turns it into themes, and gets it acted on.

Abbie Griffin and John Hauser, 1993

  1. Gather what customers say from many sources: interviews, surveys, reviews, support, sales notes.

    Consumer reviews, retailer buyer feedback and calls to the customer line.

  2. Tag each comment by product, theme and customer type.

    Tagged by meal, theme (taste, portion, price) and channel.

  3. Find the themes that come up most and matter most, and check them against numbers.

    Top theme: portions too small for dinner, in 22% of reviews.

  4. Share a short summary with the teams who can act.

    A monthly one-page summary for product, marketing and sales.

  5. Act on the top themes and tell customers what changed.

    Orto launches a larger dinner portion and replies to the reviewers.

The question it answers

What are customers telling us, and is anyone acting on it?

When to use it

  • Teams make decisions without hearing customers.
  • Feedback sits in many places and nobody reads it all.
  • Before positioning, messaging or roadmap reviews.

When to skip it

  • As a one-off survey. VoC is a habit, not a project.

How to run it

  1. List every source of customer feedback you already have.
  2. Agree a simple tagging scheme.
  3. Review themes monthly with numbers next to them.
  4. Give each top theme an owner and a deadline.
  5. Report back to customers when you act.

Common mistakes

  • Collecting without acting.
  • Letting the loudest customer stand for everyone.
  • Summaries too long for busy teams to read.
