Messaging and storytelling

Benefit Ladder

Climb from a feature to its functional benefit to its emotional benefit, then choose the rung to lead with.

Common messaging practice, rooted in means-end research

  1. What the product does or has.

    Connects to your accounting software.

  2. The practical result for the customer.

    Cash in 24 hours with no paperwork.

  3. How that result makes the customer feel.

    Calm before every payroll day.

  4. Lead with the highest rung you can credibly prove, and keep the lower rungs as proof.

    Headline: “Payroll day, without the panic.” Proof: cash in 24 hours.

The question it answers

Which level of benefit should our message lead with?

When to use it

  • Writing headlines and campaign lines.
  • Copy that lists features without saying why they matter.
  • Choosing between a rational and an emotional message.

When to skip it

  • Highly technical buyers early in evaluation, who need the functional rung first.

How to run it

  1. Start from one feature that matters to the target segment.
  2. Ask “so what?” to reach the functional benefit.
  3. Ask how that result makes the buyer feel.
  4. Choose the rung to lead with and use the others as proof.

Worked example: Kontor

Kontor is a fictional fintech that lets small businesses get paid early on their invoices.

Common mistakes

  • Emotional claims with no functional proof.
  • Benefits that any competitor could claim.
  • Laddering features nobody cares about.
