Customer understanding

Win/Loss Analysis

Ask buyers why they chose you or someone else, and use the patterns to improve how you sell.

Standard product marketing practice; no single author

  1. Pick a mix of recent wins and losses of meaningful size, decided in the last few months.

    Halden picks eight tenders decided this year: three won, five lost.

  2. Talk to the buyers, ideally through someone who was not in the sale. Sellers’ explanations are not enough.

    A neutral interviewer speaks to procurement and quality managers.

  3. Sort reasons into themes: product, price, proof, sales experience, relationship.

    Losses cite price per tonne; wins cite certified purity.

  4. Look for reasons that repeat, and for where you lose early versus late.

    Halden loses when procurement compares list price before quality is discussed.

  5. Turn patterns into changes in messaging, pricing, enablement or product, and share them widely.

    Halden adds a cost-per-good-tonne calculator to every first meeting.

The question it answers

Why do we really win and lose deals?

When to use it

  • Win rates fall or a new competitor appears.
  • Before revising positioning or pricing.
  • Building battlecards based on evidence.

When to skip it

  • With only CRM “loss reasons”, which are usually one word and often wrong.

How to run it

  1. Agree which deals qualify and who requests interviews.
  2. Use a short interview guide covering the full decision.
  3. Code answers the same way every time.
  4. Review patterns quarterly with sales, product and leadership.
  5. Track whether the changes improve win rates.

Common mistakes

  • Only interviewing losses, or only wins.
  • Accepting “price” at face value; it often hides weak value.
  • Reports that nobody acts on.

Template

WIN/LOSS INTERVIEW — [Deal], [won/lost]

How did the need start?
Which options did you consider?
What mattered most in the decision?
Where did we do well or badly?
What would have changed your decision?
