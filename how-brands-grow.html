How Brands Grow

Brands grow mainly by winning more buyers, which comes from being easy to think of and easy to buy.

Byron Sharp, Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, 2010

  1. Sharp’s research finds that bigger brands mainly have more buyers, not more loyal ones.

    Orto’s goal: more people buying it at least once a year.

  2. Grow by reaching all buyers of the category, including light and occasional buyers.

    Reach everyone who buys ready meals, not only fitness fans.

  3. Be easy to think of in buying situations, linked to the moments people need the category.

    Linked to “dinner after a late day at work”.

  4. Be easy to find and buy, wherever and whenever people shop.

    In all major chains and station shops, with shelf space.

  5. Consistent colours, shapes and characters that make the brand instantly recognisable.

    The same green tray and logo placement on every pack and ad.

  6. Rate how strong each lever is and invest in the weakest.

    Mental availability is weakest, so Orto shifts budget to broad-reach advertising.

The question it answers

Are we building the mental and physical availability that brings in more buyers?

When to use it

  • Setting marketing strategy for a consumer brand.
  • Deciding between loyalty programmes and broad reach.
  • Building a case for consistent brand assets.

When to skip it

  • Niche B2B markets with few buyers, where the patterns can differ.

How to run it

  1. Measure penetration, mental availability and physical availability.
  2. Identify the buying situations that trigger the category.
  3. Audit your distinctive assets for consistency.
  4. Invest in the weakest lever and track penetration.

Common mistakes

  • Spending most of the budget on existing loyal buyers.
  • Changing brand assets too often.
  • Narrow targeting that ignores light buyers.
