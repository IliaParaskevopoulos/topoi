Sales enablement

Solution Selling

Sell by diagnosing the buyer’s pain and building a shared picture of the solution, not by pitching the product.

Michael Bosworth, 1995

  1. Find the business problem the buyer recognises and wants to fix.

    The supermarket’s chilled ready-meal sales are flat while protein products grow.

  2. Help the buyer picture how the problem would be solved.

    A high-protein section that brings fitness shoppers into the aisle.

  3. Agree what solving it is worth to the buyer.

    A 6% lift in chilled-aisle sales in test stores.

  4. Get to the person who can approve the change.

    The head of chilled categories.

  5. Agree the steps and timing to a decision.

    A 12-week test in 20 stores, then a range review.

The question it answers

Do we understand the buyer’s pain well enough to build the solution with them?

When to use it

  • Sales where buyers do not yet know what solution they need.
  • Moving sellers from product pitches to diagnosis.

When to skip it

  • Simple, well-understood purchases.

How to run it

  1. List the main pains your product solves, per buyer role.
  2. Write diagnostic questions for each.
  3. Give sellers value examples and calculators.
  4. Coach sellers on reaching the power sponsor early.

Worked example: Orto Foods

Orto Foods is a fictional maker of chilled ready meals sold through Swiss supermarkets.

Common mistakes

  • Diagnosing for too long without moving forward.
  • Building a vision only your seller believes in.
  • Never reaching the person who can decide.
