Brand

Brand Architecture

Decide how your company brand and product names relate, from one master brand to separate brands.

David Aaker and Erich Joachimsthaler, 2000

  1. Start with the company brand and how much trust it carries with buyers.

    Tessera is known to maintenance managers as reliable and simple.

  2. Map today’s products and how they are named. Descriptive names under one master brand are a “branded house”.

    Tessera Plan and Tessera Mobile.

  3. Decide where new products fit: under the master brand, as an endorsed sub-brand, or as a separate brand.

    The new failure-prediction module.

  4. Use a branded house when the master brand helps; use separate brands when it would confuse or limit.

    Tessera keeps a branded house and names the module “Tessera Predict”.

The question it answers

How should we name new products, and how should they relate to our company brand?

When to use it

  • Naming a new product or acquired company.
  • A portfolio with inconsistent names that confuse buyers.
  • Deciding whether a product needs its own brand.

When to skip it

  • Single-product companies with no plans to expand.

How to run it

  1. List every product and how it is named today.
  2. Test with buyers how much the master brand helps each product.
  3. Pick a model: branded house, endorsed brands, or house of brands.
  4. Write simple naming rules and apply them to every new product.

Worked example: Tessera

Tessera is a fictional SaaS company whose software helps mid-size manufacturers plan and track equipment maintenance.

Common mistakes

  • Inventing a new brand for every feature.
  • Clever names that do not say what the product does.
  • Mixing models with no rule behind it.
