Customer understanding

Ideal Customer Profile

A description of the companies that get the most value from you and are most valuable to you.

Common B2B practice; no single author

  1. Begin with every company that could in theory buy.

    Every manufacturer in Germany, Austria and Switzerland.

  2. Filter by size, industry, region and structure where your best customers sit.

    Plants with 50 to 500 staff and at least three production lines.

  3. Filter by the tools and set-up that make you easy to adopt.

    No dedicated maintenance IT team; still on spreadsheets or paper.

  4. Add the events that make a company ready to buy now.

    A recent audit finding, a costly breakdown, or a new plant manager.

  5. The companies that pass every filter. Check the result against your best existing customers.

    1,400 plants, matching 9 of Tessera’s 10 most successful customers.

The question it answers

Which companies should we spend our marketing and sales effort on?

When to use it

  • Building target account lists for sales or ABM.
  • Aligning marketing and sales on which leads count.
  • Explaining why some customers churn and others grow.

When to skip it

  • Before you have enough customers to see patterns. Start with a hypothesis and revisit.

How to run it

  1. List your ten best and ten worst customers by value, retention and effort.
  2. Find what the best have in common: size, industry, tools, situation.
  3. Turn that into filters you can apply with data you can get.
  4. Size the resulting list and test it with sales.
  5. Review twice a year.

Worked example: Tessera

Tessera is a fictional SaaS company whose software helps mid-size manufacturers plan and track equipment maintenance.

Common mistakes

  • An ICP so wide it excludes nobody.
  • Confusing the ICP (companies) with personas (people).
  • Filters you cannot check with real data.
