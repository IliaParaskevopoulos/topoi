Content and channels

Hub, Hero, Hygiene

Three layers of content: always-on answers, regular series, and a few big moments.

YouTube creator guidance, around 2014

  1. The base: always-on content that answers what people search for. Useful, findable, kept up to date. YouTube later called this layer “Help”.

    Datasheets, purity FAQs and logistics guides for glass technologists.

  2. The middle: regular, scheduled content that gives people a reason to come back and follow you.

    A monthly webinar for glass technologists on raw-material quality.

  3. The top: a few big pieces a year, built to reach far beyond your usual audience.

    An annual benchmark report on sand purity across the European glass industry.

The question it answers

Do we have the right mix of everyday, regular and big-moment content?

When to use it

  • Planning a content calendar for the year.
  • Deciding how to split a content budget.
  • A content plan that is all campaigns and no everyday answers, or the reverse.

When to skip it

  • When you have not yet decided who the content is for and what you want them to do.

How to run it

  1. List what your audience searches for and asks sales; build hygiene content first.
  2. Choose one or two regular formats for hub content you can sustain.
  3. Plan one to three hero moments a year, tied to business goals.
  4. Link the layers: hero content points to hub series, hub points to hygiene detail.
  5. Measure each layer differently: search traffic, return visits, reach.

Worked example: Halden Minerals

Halden Minerals is a fictional supplier of high-purity silica sand to glass and solar-panel makers.

Common mistakes

  • Spending the whole budget on hero pieces with nothing to come back to.
  • Hub series started and abandoned after three episodes.
  • Hygiene content that is out of date.

Template

HUB, HERO, HYGIENE — [Brand], [year]

Hub (regular): format / frequency / owner
Hero (big moments): idea / timing / goal
