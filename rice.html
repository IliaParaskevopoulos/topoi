Planning and prioritization

RICE Scoring

Score ideas on reach, impact, confidence and effort, so you can compare them fairly.

Sean McBride, Intercom, around 2016

  1. How many people the idea affects in a set period. Use real numbers where you can.

    Large-invoice alerts: about 1,100 active businesses a quarter.

  2. How much it moves the goal for each person reached, on a fixed scale: 3 massive, 2 high, 1 medium, 0.5 low, 0.25 minimal.

    High: 2.

  3. How sure you are of the reach and impact estimates: 100% high, 80% medium, 50% low.

    Low: 50%, with one test so far.

  4. Total work needed from all teams, in person-months.

    3 person-months.

  5. Score = reach × impact × confidence ÷ effort. Use the score to start a discussion, not to end it.

    1,100 × 2 × 50% ÷ 3 ≈ 367. The cash-gap forecast scores 210, so alerts go first.

The question it answers

Which of our ideas will deliver the most value for the effort?

When to use it

  • Ranking a long list of ideas with limited capacity.
  • Making the reasons behind priorities visible to stakeholders.
  • Comparing ideas from different teams on one scale.

When to skip it

  • Strategic bets whose value is not captured by near-term reach.
  • When nobody can estimate reach or effort even roughly.

How to run it

  1. Agree the goal the impact score refers to.
  2. Fix the scales for impact and confidence before scoring.
  3. Estimate each idea with the people who will do the work.
  4. Calculate scores and sort the list.
  5. Discuss the top and bottom of the list; adjust where judgement disagrees, and write down why.

Common mistakes

  • Inflating confidence to push a favourite idea.
  • Forgetting effort from other teams, such as legal or support.
  • Treating the score as the decision.
