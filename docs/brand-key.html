Brand

Brand Key

A one-page brand positioning that builds from the brand’s strengths and target up to a single brand essence.

Unilever, late 1990s

  1. What the brand has always been good at.

    Real recipes and Swiss ingredients.

  2. Who and what you compete with in the buyer’s mind.

    Own-label ready meals and cooking from scratch.

  3. Who the brand is for, described by attitudes, not just demographics.

    Busy people who care what they eat.

  4. A truth about the target that the brand can act on.

    “On weekdays I want real food, but I don’t have time to cook it.”

  5. What the brand does for the target: functional and emotional.

    A proper meal in five minutes; feeling you looked after yourself.

  6. What the brand stands for and how it behaves.

    Honest, warm, no shortcuts.

  7. The proof behind the benefits.

    Small-batch kitchens, Swiss ingredients, no additives.

  8. The single most compelling reason to choose this brand.

    It tastes home-cooked.

  9. The top: the brand in a few words.

    “Real food for real weekdays.”

The question it answers

What is the essence of our brand, and what supports it?

When to use it

  • Writing or refreshing a brand positioning.
  • Briefing agencies on a consumer brand.
  • Aligning marketing teams across countries.

When to skip it

  • B2B products where buyers decide on business value; product positioning tools fit better.

How to run it

  1. Gather insight from consumer research first.
  2. Fill the key from the bottom up.
  3. Check each level supports the one above.
  4. Test the essence with the team: does it guide decisions?

Worked example: Orto Foods

Orto Foods is a fictional maker of chilled ready meals sold through Swiss supermarkets.

Root strengths
Real recipes and Swiss ingredients.
Competitive environment

Target

Insight

Benefits

Reasons to believe

Discriminator
It tastes home-cooked.
Brand essence
“Real food for real weekdays.”

Common mistakes

  • An insight that is really a product claim.
  • An essence so general it fits any brand.
  • Filling the key without consumer research.
