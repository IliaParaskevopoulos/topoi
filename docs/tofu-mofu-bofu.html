TOFU, MOFU, BOFU

Plan content for the top, middle and bottom of the funnel, so buyers find help at every stage.

Common marketing practice; no single author

  1. Divide the buying journey into top, middle and bottom.

    Nordhaus plans its content for the year.

  2. Broad, helpful content for people discovering a need. Measure reach and engagement.

    Small-flat storage ideas and moving checklists.

  3. Content that helps people compare and plan. Measure engagement and leads.

    Room planning guides and a free room-plan booking.

  4. Content that helps people decide and buy. Measure conversion.

    Delivery and assembly details, member prices, reviews.

The question it answers

Do we have content for people just discovering the problem, comparing options, and ready to buy?

When to use it

  • Planning a content calendar.
  • Content that is all top-of-funnel or all product pages.
  • Explaining content goals to stakeholders.

When to skip it

  • Complex B2B buying with many people and loops; use buying jobs or a content-to-stage map.

How to run it

  1. Sort existing content into the three stages.
  2. Set one goal and one metric per stage.
  3. Fill the biggest gaps first.
  4. Link content so readers can move to the next stage.

Common mistakes

  • Judging top-of-funnel content by sales.
  • Bottom-of-funnel content that is just a price list.
  • No links between stages.
