Go-to-market and launch

Working Backwards PR/FAQ

Write the launch press release and FAQ before building anything, to test whether the idea is worth doing.

Amazon; described by Colin Bryar and Bill Carr, 2021

  1. Name the product and the main customer benefit, in one line.

    “Nordhaus launches Room Planner: furnish a room in a weekend.”

  2. Who it is for and the benefit, in one sentence.

    For people moving home who want rooms finished fast, without mistakes.

  3. The customer problem today, in plain words.

    Moving in means weeks of shopping, measuring and returns.

  4. How the product solves it, and why it is better.

    Plan with a designer online, then one delivery with assembly.

  5. A customer quote and how to get started.

    “Our flat was done in four days.” Book at nordhaus.example.

  6. Answer the hard questions customers and the business would ask.

    What does it cost? What if I change my mind? How do we staff it?

The question it answers

If we launched this, would the announcement excite customers?

When to use it

  • Deciding whether to build a new product or feature.
  • Aligning product, marketing and leadership early.
  • Writing the launch story before development starts.

When to skip it

  • Small improvements that do not need a launch story.

How to run it

  1. Write the press release in plain language, one page.
  2. Write customer and internal FAQs.
  3. Review it with leadership and rewrite until the benefit is clear.
  4. Use it as the brief for product and launch.

Common mistakes

  • Writing it after the product is built.
  • Jargon in the press release.
  • Skipping the hard internal FAQs.
