Positioning

Category Design

Instead of competing in an existing category, define a new one around a problem, and lead it.

Al Ramadan, Dave Peterson, Christopher Lochhead and Kevin Maney, 2016

  1. Describe a problem buyers have that existing categories do not solve well.

    Antibiotics are prescribed blind because test results take days.

  2. Give the solution space a clear, simple name that frames the problem.

    “Point-of-care infection testing”.

  3. A short document that explains the problem, the new way and why now, without selling your product.

    A two-page point of view shared with GP associations.

  4. Bring in the partners and voices who make the category real: analysts, associations, integrators.

    GP associations, reimbursement bodies and lab partners.

  5. A concentrated moment that launches the category to the market, with everyone aligned.

    A joint study presented at the national GP congress.

The question it answers

Should we create and lead a new category rather than fight in an existing one?

When to use it

  • A genuinely new product that buyers keep comparing with the wrong things.
  • A company with the resources to educate a market.

When to skip it

  • An incremental product in a known category. Positioning within the category works better and costs less.

How to run it

  1. Write the problem in buyers’ terms and test it with them.
  2. Draft category names and test which one buyers understand fastest.
  3. Write the point of view and get leadership to agree it.
  4. Line up ecosystem partners before launch.
  5. Plan one concentrated launch moment rather than many small ones.

Worked example: Lumora Diagnostics

Lumora Diagnostics is a fictional company that makes a rapid blood-test reader for GP clinics.

Common mistakes

  • Inventing a category name buyers never use.
  • Calling a feature a category.
  • Underestimating the cost of educating a market.
