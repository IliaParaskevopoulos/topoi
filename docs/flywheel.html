Adoption and growth

Flywheel

A circle instead of a funnel: happy customers feed growth, so energy you put in keeps the wheel turning.

HubSpot, 2018, building on Jim Collins’s flywheel idea

  1. The customer sits in the middle. Everything the company does should keep them moving round the wheel.

    Nordhaus Home puts its loyalty members at the centre: people who shop more than twice a year.

  2. Draw people in by being useful: helpful content, local presence, being easy to find.

    Free weekend workshops in stores, and accurate local search listings with live stock.

  3. Make it easy to buy and start a relationship: good advice, smooth buying, no pressure.

    Staff who help plan a room, and click-and-collect within two hours.

  4. Help customers succeed after they buy, so they come back and tell others.

    Free delivery and assembly for members, and easy returns in any store.

  5. Force is anything that speeds the wheel up: referrals, reviews, word of mouth, well-run programmes.

    Members who leave a review get first access to sales. Reviews bring in new shoppers.

  6. Friction is anything that slows it down: handovers between teams, broken processes, poor service. Removing friction often helps more than adding force.

    Products shown online as in stock but missing on the shelf. Nordhaus fixes stock data first.

The question it answers

Where are we adding momentum to growth, and where is friction slowing it down?

When to use it

  • Growth depends heavily on repeat purchases, referrals or reviews.
  • Marketing, sales and service work in separate silos and customers feel the handovers.
  • Reframing a funnel that ignores what happens after the sale.

When to skip it

  • You need precise conversion metrics. Use a funnel model such as AARRR alongside it.
  • One-off purchases with no repeat or referral potential.

How to run it

  1. Define who sits at the centre: your best, most loyal customers.
  2. For each stage, list what you do today to attract, engage and delight.
  3. List the forces that speed the wheel up and estimate their impact.
  4. List the friction points, especially at handovers between teams.
  5. Pick the largest friction point to remove and one force to strengthen, and assign owners.

Common mistakes

  • Redrawing the funnel as a circle without changing anything in how teams work.
  • Adding force (more campaigns) while friction stays in place.
  • Leaving service and operations out of the workshop. Much of the friction sits there.
