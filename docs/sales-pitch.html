Sales Pitch (Dunford)

A pitch built on your positioning: start with an insight about the market, then show why you are the best answer.

April Dunford, 2023

  1. Open with an insight about how the market is changing and what that means for buyers.

    Plants are moving from fixed maintenance schedules to fixing what is about to fail.

  2. Describe the ways buyers can solve the problem today, and the limits of each.

    Spreadsheets miss early warnings; ERP modules need long IT projects.

  3. Describe what an ideal solution would do, based on the insight.

    Planning that warns early and needs no IT team.

  4. Introduce your product through the value only you deliver.

    Live in a day, works offline on phones, warns before parts fail.

  5. Back each value with evidence the buyer trusts.

    A pilot plant cut unplanned downtime by 18% in six months.

  6. Handle the usual concerns, then agree a clear next step.

    An IT security pack, then a two-week pilot on one line.

The question it answers

How do we turn our positioning into a pitch that helps buyers decide?

When to use it

  • Sales pitches vary from seller to seller.
  • After a repositioning, so the pitch matches it.
  • Deals stall because buyers cannot decide.

When to skip it

  • Before positioning is agreed. The pitch is built on it.

How to run it

  1. Start from your positioning: alternatives, differentiated value, best-fit customers.
  2. Write the insight that makes your value matter now.
  3. Build the pitch in this order and keep slides few.
  4. Practise with sales and refine after real calls.

Worked example: Tessera

Tessera is a fictional SaaS company whose software helps mid-size manufacturers plan and track equipment maintenance.

Objections and next step
An IT security pack, then a two-week pilot on one line.

Common mistakes

  • Opening with company history.
  • An insight that is really a product claim.
  • Attacking competitors instead of explaining the limits of the alternatives.
