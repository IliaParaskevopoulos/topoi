Sales enablement

Challenger Sale

Win by teaching buyers something new about their business, then showing how you solve it.

Matthew Dixon and Brent Adamson, CEB, 2011

  1. Build credibility by showing you understand the buyer’s challenges, often from what peers face.

    Solar-glass makers are pushing furnaces harder to meet demand.

  2. Share an insight that changes how the buyer sees the problem.

    Most rejected batches start with small purity swings in the sand, not with the furnace.

  3. Back the reframe with data that shows its size.

    Purity swings explain about half of rejects in Halden’s customer data.

  4. Make it personal: how the problem affects people like them.

    The quality manager is blamed for stoppages they could not see coming.

  5. Describe the better approach, before naming your product.

    Certify purity for every shipment, not batch averages.

  6. Only now show how your product delivers the new way.

    Halden’s per-shipment certificates and replacement guarantee.

The question it answers

What insight can we teach buyers that reframes how they see their problem?

When to use it

  • Complex B2B sales where buyers think they know what they need.
  • Building insight-led content and pitches.
  • Markets where products look similar.

When to skip it

  • When you have no real insight. A fake reframe damages trust.

How to run it

  1. Find an insight from your data that buyers do not know.
  2. Build the six-step sequence around it.
  3. Create the supporting content: data, stories, a simple model.
  4. Train sellers and practise the reframe.

Halden Minerals is a fictional supplier of high-purity silica sand to glass and solar-panel makers.

Common mistakes

  • Being challenging without being helpful.
  • An insight that leads to any competitor’s product.
  • Skipping straight to the solution.
