Sales enablement

SPIN Selling

Four kinds of question that move a buyer from describing their situation to stating the value of a solution.

Neil Rackham, 1988

  1. Questions about facts and context. Necessary, but keep them few: buyers tire of them.

    How do you test for infections today, and how long do results take?

  2. Questions about difficulties and dissatisfaction with the current way.

    How often do you prescribe antibiotics while waiting for a result?

  3. Questions about the consequences of the problem, which make it bigger and more urgent.

    What do unnecessary prescriptions cost you in follow-up visits and patient trust?

  4. Questions that get the buyer to state the value of solving it, in their own words.

    If you had a result within ten minutes, how would that change your consultations?

  5. Rackham’s research found that successful sellers ask more implication and need-payoff questions, and fewer situation questions.

    Lumora’s call guide has two situation questions, then most time on implications and payoff.

The question it answers

What should sellers ask so the customer sees the size of the problem and the value of solving it?

When to use it

  • Larger B2B sales where the buyer must justify a change.
  • Building discovery guides with sales.
  • Training new sellers.

When to skip it

  • Small, quick purchases where long questioning slows the sale.
  • As a script read word for word.

How to run it

  1. List the problems your product solves for each buyer role.
  2. For each problem, write implication questions about cost, risk and time.
  3. Write need-payoff questions that let the buyer state the value.
  4. Keep situation questions few; research the rest before the call.
  5. Practise in role-play and listen to recorded calls.

Worked example: Lumora Diagnostics

Lumora Diagnostics is a fictional company that makes a rapid blood-test reader for GP clinics.

Common mistakes

  • Too many situation questions, which bore the buyer.
  • Jumping to the pitch before the buyer feels the implication.
  • Implication questions that sound like manipulation. Stay genuine.
