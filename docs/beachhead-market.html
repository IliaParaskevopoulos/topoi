Go-to-market and launch

Beachhead Market

Choose one small market to win completely first, then use it as a base to expand.

Bill Aulet, Disciplined Entrepreneurship, 2013

  1. Brainstorm all the segments that could use the product.

    Small businesses in Switzerland: construction, IT, cleaning, events and more.

  2. Score each on criteria: money to spend, easy to reach, a strong reason to buy, the whole product ready, weak competition, links to other markets.

    Construction subcontractors, IT freelancers and cleaning firms.

  3. Pick one market small enough to dominate and close-knit enough for word of mouth.

    Construction subcontractors: paid in 60–90 days, weekly payroll, tight networks.

  4. Win and delight the first customers, so they bring the rest of the market.

    30 subcontractors in Zurich in the first six months.

The question it answers

Which single market should we win first?

When to use it

  • A new product with many possible markets.
  • A start-up spreading itself too thin.
  • Entering a new country.

When to skip it

  • Mature products that already lead several markets.

How to run it

  1. List markets broadly, then interview a few customers in each.
  2. Score markets on the criteria.
  3. Choose one, even if it feels small.
  4. Define what “winning” the beachhead means, in numbers.

Worked example: Kontor

Kontor is a fictional fintech that lets small businesses get paid early on their invoices.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing the biggest market instead of the most winnable.
  • Picking two beachheads “to be safe”.
  • A beachhead with no links to follow-on markets.
