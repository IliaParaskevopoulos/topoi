Competitive Intelligence Cycle

A repeating loop that turns questions about competitors into information people use to decide.

Adapted from the intelligence cycle by SCIP practitioners

  1. Start with the decisions intelligence should support, and the key questions to answer.

    “Will the large lab chain launch a rival rapid test this year?”

  2. Gather information from public sources, sales, customers, partners and events.

    Tender documents, trade shows, regulatory filings and sales call notes.

  3. Turn information into meaning: what changed, why it matters, what is likely next.

    Their job adverts for diagnostics engineers suggest a launch within 12 months.

  4. Deliver it in the format each audience uses: briefs, battlecards, alerts.

    A monthly brief for leadership and updated battlecards for sales.

  5. The centre of the loop. Intelligence only matters when it informs a decision; then new questions start the next round.

    Lumora speeds up its paediatric launch to get ahead of the rival test.

The question it answers

Are we learning about competitors in a way that actually changes our decisions?

When to use it

  • Setting up or fixing a competitive intelligence programme.
  • Competitive information exists but nobody uses it.
  • A competitor move caught the company by surprise.

When to skip it

  • As a reason to collect everything about everyone. Start from questions.

How to run it

  1. Agree three to five key intelligence questions with leadership and sales.
  2. List sources for each question and who collects them.
  3. Analyse monthly and write what it means, not just what happened.
  4. Share in short formats tied to decisions, and ask what changed as a result.

Common mistakes

  • Newsletters of competitor news with no analysis.
  • Collecting information nobody asked for.
  • Ignoring what sales hear every day.
