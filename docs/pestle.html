Market and competitive analysis

PESTLE

Scan six kinds of outside change that could open or close opportunities for your product.

Francis Aguilar, 1967 (as ETPS); later extended to PESTLE

  1. Government policy, trade rules, subsidies and political stability.

    EU programmes to build solar manufacturing in Europe.

  2. Growth, inflation, interest rates, exchange rates and input costs.

    Energy prices drive quarrying and drying costs.

  3. Demographics, attitudes, skills and ways of working.

    Fewer young people training as quarry technicians.

  4. New technologies that change products, processes or buying.

    Thinner solar glass needs even purer sand.

  5. Laws and regulation that affect the product, sales or operations.

    Stricter extraction permits and land-restoration rules.

  6. Climate, resources and environmental rules and expectations.

    Limits on water use and dust at quarries.

  7. Rate each factor by impact and likelihood, and keep only the few that change your plans.

    Halden plans around three: solar demand, energy costs and purity requirements.

The question it answers

Which outside forces will change our market in the next few years?

When to use it

  • Entering a new country or industry.
  • Annual planning, to check that outside changes are covered.
  • Explaining to leadership why a market will grow or shrink.

When to skip it

  • Short-term campaign planning, where these forces barely move.

How to run it

  1. Agree the market and time horizon, usually three to five years.
  2. Research each factor and note the evidence.
  3. Rate impact and likelihood for each one.
  4. Keep the top three to five and turn them into opportunities or risks in your plan.

Common mistakes

  • Long lists of general trends with no link to your product.
  • No evidence behind the points.
  • Doing it once and never updating it.
