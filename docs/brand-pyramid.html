Brand

Brand Pyramid (CBBE)

Brand strength is built in four steps, from being known at all to customers feeling a real bond with you.

Kevin Lane Keller, 1993 and 2001

  1. The base: how easily customers think of you, and in which buying situations. Without salience nothing above it matters.

    When people think of furnishing a room on a budget, Nordhaus comes to mind, helped by stores in 35 town centres.

  2. How well the product meets functional needs: reliability, durability, service, price, design.

    Furniture that lasts, honest prices and delivery on the promised day.

  3. The less tangible side: who uses the brand, in which situations, and what personality and values it has.

    Warm, practical homes, and the store where staff help you plan a whole room.

  4. What customers conclude about the brand: quality, credibility, whether to consider it, and whether it is better than others.

    Customers judge it good value and trustworthy, and rate the staff advice highly.

  5. How the brand makes customers feel: warmth, fun, security, social approval, self-respect.

    Shopping there feels relaxed and reassuring rather than overwhelming.

  6. The top: loyalty, attachment, community and active engagement. Customers buy again, join in and recommend you.

    Members come back several times a year, join workshops and recommend Nordhaus to friends.

The question it answers

How strong is our brand in customers’ minds, and which level do we need to build next?

When to use it

  • Diagnosing why a brand that is well known still does not win enough loyalty.
  • Planning brand work in the right order, from awareness up.
  • Aligning marketing, sales and leadership on what the brand should stand for.

When to skip it

  • You need a short-term demand plan. The pyramid is about long-term brand strength.
  • Without research. Filling it from internal opinion tells you what you hope, not what customers think.

How to run it

  1. Research each level with customers: awareness, perceptions, judgments, feelings and loyalty.
  2. Fill the pyramid with what customers actually say, level by level.
  3. Write the target state for each level next to the current one.
  4. Find the lowest level with a gap. Work above a weak level rarely sticks.
  5. Plan activities for that level, and measure it again in a year.

Worked example: Nordhaus Home

Nordhaus Home is a fictional chain of 35 home-goods stores.

Common mistakes

  • Starting at resonance with community programmes before customers know what the brand stands for.
  • Treating performance and imagery as one. A brand can be strong on one and weak on the other.
  • Writing what the company wants to be, not what customers believe today.
