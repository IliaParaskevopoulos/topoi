Positioning

Positioning (Ries and Trout)

Positioning happens in the buyer’s mind: find your place on the mental ladder for your category, and own one idea.

Al Ries and Jack Trout, 1972; book 1981

  1. Buyers keep a mental ranking of brands in each category. Identify the category ladder you are on.

    Ready meals in Swiss supermarkets.

  2. Be honest about where buyers place you. The leader owns the category; others need a different idea.

    Own-label meals lead; a big international brand is second; Orto is third.

  3. Choose one idea or word that matters to buyers and that no competitor owns, and repeat it everywhere.

    Orto chooses “high-protein”.

  4. If you cannot be first on an existing ladder, create a new category where you can be first.

    “High-protein ready meals”, a ladder where Orto is first.

The question it answers

What single idea can we own in buyers’ minds?

When to use it

  • A crowded market where buyers see little difference.
  • Simplifying a message that tries to say too much.
  • Deciding whether to fight the leader or create a new category.

When to skip it

  • Complex B2B purchases decided on many criteria, where one word is not enough on its own.

How to run it

  1. Research how buyers rank brands in your category.
  2. List ideas buyers care about and which brand owns each.
  3. Pick one free idea you can credibly own.
  4. Make every message and product choice reinforce it.

Worked example: Orto Foods

Orto Foods is a fictional maker of chilled ready meals sold through Swiss supermarkets.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing an idea buyers do not care about.
  • Claiming the leader’s idea.
  • Changing the idea every year.
