Brand

Brand Identity Prism

Six facets that together define a brand’s identity, from how it looks to how customers see themselves when using it.

Jean-Noël Kapferer, 1992

  1. The brand’s physical features: products, logo, colours, stores.

    Light wood, plain textiles, bright stores in town centres.

  2. The character the brand shows in how it speaks and behaves.

    Practical, warm and quietly confident.

  3. The values and origin the brand draws on.

    Nordic simplicity: good design should be affordable.

  4. The kind of relationship the brand offers customers.

    A helpful neighbour who knows about homes.

  5. The image of the typical customer the brand projects.

    Young families and couples making a first real home.

  6. How customers see themselves when they use the brand.

    “I have a home I’m proud of, without overspending.”

  7. All six facets should tell the same story. The brand sits at the centre.

    Nordhaus finds its stores feel more premium than its prices; it changes store signage.

The question it answers

Is our brand identity complete and consistent across all six facets?

When to use it

  • Defining a new brand or refreshing an old one.
  • Briefing agencies.
  • Checking brand consistency across channels.

When to skip it

  • Product-level positioning, which needs alternatives and differentiated value.

How to run it

  1. Fill each facet from customer research and current materials.
  2. Mark where facets contradict each other.
  3. Decide the target identity for each facet.
  4. Update guidelines and the touchpoints that do not fit.

Worked example: Nordhaus Home

Nordhaus Home is a fictional chain of 35 home-goods stores.

Common mistakes

  • Describing the brand you wish you had.
  • Facets that contradict each other.
  • Confusing reflection (who the brand shows) with the actual target customer.
