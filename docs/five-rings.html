Customer understanding

5 Rings of Buying Insight

A buyer persona built from what buyers actually say about their decision, in five parts.

Adele Revella, Buyer Persona Institute, 2015

  1. What made the buyer decide to invest time and money in solving this problem now.

    New national guidance on antibiotic prescribing, and patients asking for faster answers.

  2. The results the buyer expects from a good decision.

    Fewer return visits, and a clear decision during the consultation.

  3. What makes the buyer doubt you or your type of solution.

    Doubts about accuracy compared with the lab, staff time, and cost.

  4. The specific features and attributes the buyer compares, in their words.

    Validated accuracy, a reimbursement code, and a result within a ten-minute consult.

  5. Who is involved, which sources they trust, and the steps they take to decide.

    Starts after a guideline update; owners ask peer GPs, run a trial, then partners decide.

The question it answers

What do our buyers need to hear, and when, to choose us?

When to use it

  • Building or replacing buyer personas.
  • Briefing content and sales teams on what buyers need to hear.
  • Finding why deals stall at a particular stage.

When to skip it

  • From desk research only. The method depends on buyer interviews.

How to run it

  1. Interview buyers who decided in the last few months, including those who chose a competitor.
  2. Ask them to tell the story from the moment they started looking.
  3. Code their answers into the five rings.
  4. Write the persona in buyers’ own words and share it with sales and content.

Worked example: Lumora Diagnostics

Lumora Diagnostics is a fictional company that makes a rapid blood-test reader for GP clinics.

Common mistakes

  • Filling the rings with internal opinions.
  • Demographic details that do not change what you say.
  • Interviewing only happy customers.
