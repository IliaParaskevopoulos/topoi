Brand

Brand and Activation Balance

Activation brings short sales spikes; brand building grows sales slowly but lasts. Effective budgets do both.

Les Binet and Peter Field, IPA, 2013

  1. Targeted, rational messages that drive sales now. The effect is quick but fades.

    Search ads and offers for business owners looking for cash today.

  2. Broad, emotional, memorable messages. The effect builds slowly and lasts.

    A recurring campaign: “Get paid like the big companies do.”

  3. Binet and Field found the best long-term results with roughly 60% brand and 40% activation in consumer markets; later B2B work suggests closer to half and half. Treat these as starting points.

    Kontor moves from 10% to 40% brand spend over two years.

The question it answers

Are we balancing short-term sales activation with long-term brand building?

When to use it

  • Setting the annual marketing budget.
  • Leadership pushes all spend into short-term lead generation.
  • Growth has stalled despite strong performance marketing.

When to skip it

  • Very early start-ups that must prove demand before building a brand.

How to run it

  1. Classify current spend as brand building or activation.
  2. Compare with the reference splits for your market.
  3. Move gradually and keep brand messages consistent for years.
  4. Measure brand effects over quarters, not weeks.

Worked example: Kontor

Kontor is a fictional fintech that lets small businesses get paid early on their invoices.

Common mistakes

  • Cutting brand spend because results are slow to show.
  • Brand campaigns that change every year.
  • Treating 60/40 as a rule rather than a starting point.
