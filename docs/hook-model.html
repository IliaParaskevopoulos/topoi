Growth and lifecycle

Hook Model

Four steps that, repeated, turn using a product into a habit: trigger, action, variable reward, investment.

Nir Eyal, 2014

  1. What prompts the behaviour. External triggers are messages and reminders; internal triggers are feelings or routines.

    A message about a storage workshop this Saturday, or the feeling that the flat is cluttered.

  2. The simplest thing the user does in expectation of a reward. The easier it is, the more likely it happens.

    Open the app and save a room idea.

  3. A reward that changes each time, which keeps interest. It can be social, material or personal.

    New room ideas each week, and member prices that change.

  4. The user puts something in, such as data, content or effort, that makes the product better for them and loads the next trigger.

    Uploading room sizes and a photo, so the next suggestions fit.

  5. After enough loops, the internal trigger alone brings the user back. Measure how often users return without a prompt.

    Members open the app weekly whenever they plan a change at home.

The question it answers

What brings customers back to our product without us having to ask every time?

When to use it

  • Designing digital products, apps or programmes meant for repeated use.
  • Usage drops after sign-up and you need to know where the loop breaks.
  • Planning lifecycle messages that lead to a habit, not just a click.

When to skip it

  • Products used rarely by nature, such as annual purchases.
  • Any design that exploits users against their interest. Eyal himself warns against it.

How to run it

  1. Identify the internal trigger: the feeling or moment when users need you.
  2. Make the first action as simple as possible.
  3. Design a reward that varies and is worth coming back for.
  4. Ask for a small investment that improves the next visit.
  5. Measure loop by loop: trigger response, action rate, return without prompt.

Worked example: Nordhaus Home

Nordhaus Home is a fictional chain of 35 home-goods stores.

Trigger
A message about a storage workshop this Saturday, or the feeling that the flat is cluttered.
Action
Open the app and save a room idea.
Variable reward
New room ideas each week, and member prices that change.
Investment
Uploading room sizes and a photo, so the next suggestions fit.
A habit forms
Members open the app weekly whenever they plan a change at home.

Common mistakes

  • Relying on push messages forever instead of earning an internal trigger.
  • Rewards that never change.
  • Building habits that do not serve the user, which damages trust and invites regulation.
