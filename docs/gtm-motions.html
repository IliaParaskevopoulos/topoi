Go-to-Market Motions

Choose how customers first buy: through the product itself, through salespeople, or a mix, depending on deal size.

Common software go-to-market practice; no single author

  1. Left to right: deal size. Bottom to top: who leads the first sale, the sales team or the product.

    Tessera maps its customers by plant size and how they first bought.

  2. Small deals where buyers try and buy on their own.

    Single-line workshops trying the free plan.

  3. Larger deals that start with product use, then sales steps in using usage signals.

    Plants that start a pilot on one line, then roll out to the site.

  4. Smaller deals that still need a conversation, often by phone or video.

    Plants under 100 staff with one maintenance manager.

  5. Large, complex deals with account teams.

    Groups with five or more plants.

  6. Most companies mix motions; the key is choosing one per segment and supporting it with the right marketing.

    Tessera’s main motion is product-led sales: pilots first, then a site licence.

The question it answers

Should customers buy through the product, through sales, or both?

When to use it

  • Customer acquisition costs are too high for deal sizes.
  • Adding a free plan or a sales team.
  • Different segments buy in very different ways.

When to skip it

  • Before you know how your best customers actually bought.

How to run it

  1. Map recent deals by size and how they started.
  2. Find which motion wins each segment most efficiently.
  3. Choose a primary motion and supporting ones.
  4. Align marketing: self-serve content, usage alerts, or account-based programmes.

Common mistakes

  • Adding a free plan without a path to paid.
  • Expensive field sales on small deals.
  • Running every motion at once with no focus.
