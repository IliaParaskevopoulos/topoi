Go-to-market and launch

Marketing Mix: 4Ps and 7Ps

The decisions that bring a product to market: product, price, place and promotion, plus three more for services.

E. Jerome McCarthy, 1960 (4Ps); Bernard Booms and Mary Jo Bitner, 1981 (7Ps)

  1. What you offer: features, quality, range, packaging.

    A high-protein range of five meals, 400 g each.

  2. Price level, discounts and terms.

    CHF 7.90, above own-label, below premium brands.

  3. Where and how customers can buy.

    Two supermarket chains, plus railway-station shops.

  4. How customers learn about it: advertising, PR, in-store, digital.

    In-store tastings and fitness-app partnerships.

  5. For services: the people who deliver the experience.

    Trained promoters at tastings.

  6. For services: how the service is delivered.

    Fresh delivery to stores three times a week.

  7. For services: what customers see that signals quality.

    Clear protein labels and a recyclable tray.

  8. Every P should support the same positioning and target segment.

    Station shops fit busy, fitness-focused singles; discount chains would not.

The question it answers

Have we made a deliberate, consistent decision on every part of the offer?

When to use it

  • Checking that a launch plan covers every decision.
  • Explaining the plan to people new to marketing.
  • Reviewing whether each element supports the positioning.

When to skip it

  • Complex B2B solutions, where SAVE often fits better.

How to run it

  1. Start from the target segment and positioning.
  2. Make a decision for each P, in one or two lines.
  3. Check every P against the positioning.
  4. Fix the elements that do not fit.

Worked example: Orto Foods

Orto Foods is a fictional maker of chilled ready meals sold through Swiss supermarkets.

Product
A high-protein range of five meals, 400 g each.
Price
CHF 7.90, above own-label, below premium brands.
Place
Two supermarket chains, plus railway-station shops.
Promotion
In-store tastings and fitness-app partnerships.
People
Trained promoters at tastings.
Process
Fresh delivery to stores three times a week.
Physical evidence
Clear protein labels and a recyclable tray.
Check the fit
Station shops fit busy, fitness-focused singles; discount chains would not.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the Ps as separate team plans.
  • A price or place that contradicts the positioning.
  • Leaving out the service Ps when service is part of the offer.
