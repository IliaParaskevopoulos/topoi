Content and channels

They Ask, You Answer

Answer the questions buyers really ask, openly, starting with five topics most companies avoid.

Marcus Sheridan, 2017

  1. Explain what things cost and why, even if you cannot give an exact price.

    “How much does a rapid test reader cost a GP practice?”

  2. Be honest about the problems and limits of your type of product.

    “When rapid infection tests are not the right choice.”

  3. Compare options fairly, including yours versus alternatives.

    “Rapid test or lab test: which is better for your practice?”

  4. Review products and approaches in your category, including others’.

    “An honest review of rapid CRP tests for primary care.”

  5. Lists of the best options for specific needs, even if you are not on every list.

    “The best point-of-care tests for paediatric practices.”

The question it answers

Are we answering the questions buyers actually ask before they talk to us?

When to use it

  • Building trust in a considered purchase.
  • Sales calls spend too long on basic questions.
  • Website content that does not attract buyers.

When to skip it

  • When legal or regulatory rules stop you discussing prices or comparisons openly; check first.

How to run it

  1. Collect the questions sales and support hear most.
  2. Start with the five topics, in buyers’ words.
  3. Publish honestly, including limits.
  4. Give sellers the articles to send before and after calls.

Common mistakes

  • Avoiding price because it feels risky.
  • Comparisons that are really adverts.
  • Writing for search engines instead of buyers.
