Positioning

Positioning Statement

One structured sentence that says who the product is for, what it is, why it matters and how it differs.

Geoffrey Moore, 1991

  1. Who the product is for. Narrow enough that you could list real companies.

    For solar-glass makers who need consistently low-iron sand.

  2. The problem or opportunity that makes them look for a solution.

    Who lose output when impurities vary between deliveries.

  3. The market frame you compete in. It tells buyers what to compare you with.

    Halden is a high-purity silica supplier.

  4. The main reason to buy, as an outcome for the customer.

    That guarantees iron below 100 ppm in every delivery, tested and certified.

  5. The option the customer would otherwise choose.

    Unlike general glass-sand quarries that test only batch averages.

  6. What makes you different from that alternative, and can be proven.

    Every shipment comes with its own certificate and a replacement guarantee.

The question it answers

Can we say, in one sentence, who we are for and why we are the better choice?

When to use it

  • After positioning work, to record the result in one place.
  • Checking that a new website, deck or campaign matches the positioning.
  • Onboarding new staff and agencies.

When to skip it

  • As customer-facing copy. It is an internal tool and reads awkwardly out loud.
  • To decide the positioning. Do that first, with a method such as Obviously Awesome.

How to run it

  1. Fill each slot with evidence from customers and win/loss data, not hopes.
  2. Make the target customer narrow enough to name real accounts.
  3. Write the alternative the customer would really choose, which may not be your biggest competitor.
  4. Test the statement with sales: does it match the deals you win?
  5. Use it as a filter for every message you write.

Worked example: Halden Minerals

Halden Minerals is a fictional supplier of high-purity silica sand to glass and solar-panel makers.

Target customer
For solar-glass makers who need consistently low-iron sand.
The need
Who lose output when impurities vary between deliveries.
Category
Halden is a high-purity silica supplier.
Key benefit
That guarantees iron below 100 ppm in every delivery, tested and certified.
Main alternative
Unlike general glass-sand quarries that test only batch averages.
Difference
Every shipment comes with its own certificate and a replacement guarantee.

Common mistakes

  • A target customer so broad it includes everyone.
  • A benefit that is really a feature.
  • A difference you cannot prove.

Template

POSITIONING STATEMENT — [Product]

For [target customer]
who [need or opportunity],
[product] is a [market category]
that [key benefit].
Unlike [main alternative],
our product [main difference].
