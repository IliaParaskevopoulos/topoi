Go-to-market and launch

Account-Based Marketing Tiers

Match marketing effort to account value: broad programmes for many, tailored ones for a few, and fully custom work for the most important.

ITSMA, early 2000s

  1. Many accounts with lighter tailoring: targeted ads and content by industry.

    200 glass and ceramics plants: targeted ads and industry content.

  2. Clusters of similar accounts with shared needs: tailored events and content per cluster.

    20 solar-glass makers: a purity workshop and tailored case studies.

  3. A handful of strategic accounts, each with its own plan, research and executive contact.

    Five strategic accounts: account plans, custom yield analysis and executive meetings.

  4. Set the tiers by account value and potential, and review them every quarter.

    Accounts move up a tier when they start a lab qualification.

The question it answers

How much tailored marketing does each target account deserve?

When to use it

  • B2B with a limited number of high-value accounts.
  • Marketing and sales disagree about which accounts matter.
  • Large deals that need many contacts at once.

When to skip it

  • High-volume, low-value sales.

How to run it

  1. Build the account list from your ICP.
  2. Score accounts by value and readiness.
  3. Assign tiers and define the programme for each.
  4. Plan jointly with sales and review quarterly.

Worked example: Halden Minerals

Halden Minerals is a fictional supplier of high-purity silica sand to glass and solar-panel makers.

Common mistakes

  • Too many one-to-one accounts to serve properly.
  • ABM run by marketing alone.
  • Measuring leads instead of account progress.
