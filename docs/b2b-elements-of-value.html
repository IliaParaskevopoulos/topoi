Messaging and storytelling

B2B Elements of Value

A pyramid of the kinds of value B2B buyers look for, from basics to personal and inspirational value.

Eric Almquist, Jamie Cleghorn and Lori Sherer, Bain and Company, 2018

  1. The base: meeting specifications, an acceptable price, compliance. Necessary, but not a reason to choose you.

    Meets the specification, fair price, regulatory compliance.

  2. Economic and performance value: lower costs, better quality, higher output.

    Higher furnace output and fewer rejected batches.

  3. Productivity, operations, access, relationship and strategic fit.

    Reliable delivery and clear certificates for every shipment.

  4. What the buyer gains personally: less anxiety, better reputation, career growth.

    The quality manager no longer fears an audit or a line stoppage.

  5. The top: vision, hope and social responsibility.

    Helping Europe build its own solar supply chain.

The question it answers

Which kinds of value do we deliver beyond the basics, and do we say so?

When to use it

  • A message that only talks about price and specifications.
  • Finding why customers stay with you even when cheaper options exist.
  • Writing messages for different members of the buying group.

When to skip it

  • When you have not yet proved functional value. The upper levels rest on it.

How to run it

  1. Interview customers about why they chose you and why they stay.
  2. Sort what they say into the five levels.
  3. Compare with what competitors deliver.
  4. Add the strongest higher-level value to your messaging, with proof.

Worked example: Halden Minerals

Halden Minerals is a fictional supplier of high-purity silica sand to glass and solar-panel makers.

Table stakes

Inspirational value
Helping Europe build its own solar supply chain.

Common mistakes

  • Claiming inspirational value without functional proof.
  • Listing every element instead of choosing a few.
  • Ignoring individual value, which often decides B2B purchases.
