Value Proposition Formula

One sentence: who you help, what you help them do, how, and compared with what.

Steve Blank’s “We help X do Y by doing Z”

  1. The specific customer you help.

    We help people moving into a new home…

  2. The outcome they want, in their words.

    …furnish their main rooms in a weekend…

  3. How you deliver it: your approach, not a feature list.

    …by planning the rooms with them, then delivering and assembling everything…

  4. Optional: the alternative you are better than.

    …unlike big-box stores where you do it all yourself.

The question it answers

Can we say clearly who we help, with what, and how?

When to use it

  • The first line of a pitch, website or LinkedIn profile.
  • Testing whether a team agrees on what the product is for.
  • Early-stage products that need a clear, simple statement.

When to skip it

  • As a replacement for full positioning work in a crowded market.

How to run it

  1. Write several versions with different customers and outcomes.
  2. Pick the most specific one that is still true.
  3. Read it to target customers and watch whether they nod.
  4. Use it as the first line everywhere.

Worked example: Nordhaus Home

Nordhaus Home is a fictional chain of 35 home-goods stores.

Who
We help people moving into a new home…
Do what
…furnish their main rooms in a weekend…
By how
…by planning the rooms with them, then delivering and assembling everything…
Unlike
…unlike big-box stores where you do it all yourself.

Common mistakes

  • A “who” that means everyone.
  • An outcome that is really a feature.
  • A “how” full of jargon.

Template

VALUE PROPOSITION — [Product]

We help [specific customer]
to [outcome]
by [how]
unlike [alternative].
