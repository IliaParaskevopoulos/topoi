Positioning

Onlyness Statement

Complete the sentence “we are the only…”. If you cannot, you are not different enough yet.

Marty Neumeier, Zag, 2006

  1. The category you compete in, with “the only” in front of it.

    Our product is the only high-purity silica supplier…

  2. What makes you different from everyone else in that category.

    that certifies every shipment, not just batch averages…

  3. The customers you serve.

    for solar-glass makers…

  4. The geography you serve.

    in Europe…

  5. The need that makes the difference matter.

    who lose output when sand quality varies…

  6. The trend or moment that makes it urgent.

    as Europe rebuilds its own solar supply chain.

The question it answers

Can we honestly say we are the only one that does this, for these people?

When to use it

  • Checking whether a positioning is truly distinct.
  • Aligning founders and leaders on what makes the company different.
  • Before naming or category work.

When to skip it

  • As a tagline. It is a test and an internal anchor, not copy.

How to run it

  1. Write the sentence alone, then compare drafts as a team.
  2. Challenge every “only” against real competitors.
  3. Narrow the who or where until the “only” is true.
  4. Use the result to check messaging and product decisions.

Worked example: Halden Minerals

Halden Minerals is a fictional supplier of high-purity silica sand to glass and solar-panel makers.

Common mistakes

  • An “only” that a competitor could also claim.
  • Stretching the sentence until it loses meaning.
  • Ignoring the result when it shows you are not different.

Template

ONLYNESS STATEMENT — [Brand]

We are the only [category]
that [difference]
for [customers]
in [geography]
who [need]
during [trend].
