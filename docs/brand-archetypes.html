Brand Archetypes

Twelve recognisable characters a brand can embody, grouped by the basic motivation each one speaks to.

Margaret Mark and Carol Pearson, 2001, drawing on Carl Jung

  1. Archetypes for the need to find fulfilment and freedom: the Innocent, the Explorer and the Sage.

    The Sage, who seeks the truth, fits a brand built on evidence.

  2. Archetypes for the need to achieve and change things: the Hero, the Outlaw and the Magician.

    The Hero would suit a brand about winning a fight against infection.

  3. Archetypes for the need to connect with others: the Everyman, the Lover and the Jester.

    The Everyman would make Lumora feel friendly but less expert.

  4. Archetypes for the need for order and security: the Caregiver, the Creator and the Ruler.

    The Caregiver fits the patient side, but GPs buy on evidence.

  5. Pick one primary archetype that fits your customers’ motivation and your proof, and let it guide voice, design and behaviour. Treat it as a creative tool, not a science.

    Lumora chooses the Sage: calm, clear, evidence first, in every message.

The question it answers

What character should our brand play, so its voice and behaviour feel consistent?

When to use it

  • Defining brand voice and tone.
  • Briefing designers, writers and agencies.
  • A brand that sounds different in every channel.

When to skip it

  • As a substitute for positioning. The archetype shapes how you speak, not what you claim.
  • As evidence about customers; it is a creative framework, not research.

How to run it

  1. Agree the customer motivation your brand serves.
  2. Shortlist two or three archetypes that fit it.
  3. Test them against your proof and your competitors’ archetypes.
  4. Choose one and write voice and design guidelines from it.

Worked example: Lumora Diagnostics

Lumora Diagnostics is a fictional company that makes a rapid blood-test reader for GP clinics.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing an archetype the company cannot live up to.
  • Mixing several archetypes equally.
  • Picking the same archetype as your main competitor.
