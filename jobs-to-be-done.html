Customer understanding

Jobs-to-be-Done

Customers do not buy products; they “hire” them to make progress in a specific situation.

Clayton Christensen and colleagues, 2003; job stories from Intercom, around 2013

  1. Start with the circumstance that triggers the need. The same person has different jobs in different situations.

    When I’ve just moved into a new flat and the rooms are empty.

  2. What the customer wants to do in that situation, in their words, without naming a product.

    I want to get the basics in place within a weekend.

  3. The progress they expect: what will be better once the job is done.

    So I can feel at home and invite friends over.

  4. The practical task to get done, which you can usually measure.

    Furnish a living room and bedroom quickly, within budget.

  5. How they want to feel, and how they want to be seen by others. These often decide the purchase.

    Feel settled, and not be embarrassed when people visit.

  6. The current solutions, including doing nothing. These are your real competitors.

    A weekend at a big-box store, second-hand marketplaces, or living with boxes for weeks.

The question it answers

What progress is the customer trying to make, and what are they hiring today to make it?

When to use it

  • Finding out why customers really buy, beyond features.
  • Finding competitors you did not know you had.
  • Writing positioning and messaging in the customer’s terms.

When to skip it

  • When you need a demographic profile for media buying. Jobs describe situations, not audiences.
  • Without talking to customers. Jobs written at a desk are guesses.

How to run it

  1. Interview people who bought (or switched) in the last few months.
  2. Ask about the moment they started looking and what pushed them.
  3. Write job statements in their words: situation, motivation, outcome.
  4. Note functional, emotional and social sides, and what else they considered.
  5. Group similar jobs and choose the ones your product serves best.

Worked example: Nordhaus Home

Nordhaus Home is a fictional chain of 35 home-goods stores.

The situation
When I’ve just moved into a new flat and the rooms are empty.
The motivation
I want to get the basics in place within a weekend.
The outcome
So I can feel at home and invite friends over.
The functional job
Furnish a living room and bedroom quickly, within budget.
Emotional and social jobs
Feel settled, and not be embarrassed when people visit.
What they hire today
A weekend at a big-box store, second-hand marketplaces, or living with boxes for weeks.

Common mistakes

  • Writing the job around your product (“I want to use Nordhaus…”).
  • Ignoring emotional and social jobs, which often decide the purchase.
  • Treating personas and jobs as the same thing.
