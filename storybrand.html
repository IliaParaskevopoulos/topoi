Messaging and storytelling

StoryBrand

Tell your marketing as a story where the customer is the hero and your company is the guide.

Donald Miller, 2017

  1. The customer is the hero, with something they want.

    Someone moving into a new flat.

  2. Name the external problem, and how it makes them feel.

    Empty rooms, no time, and fear of costly mistakes.

  3. Your company, showing empathy and authority.

    Nordhaus room planners who have furnished thousands of homes.

  4. A few simple steps that make buying feel safe.

    Book a free plan, choose your pieces, we deliver and assemble.

  5. A clear, direct call to act.

    “Book a free room plan.”

  6. What is at stake if they do nothing.

    Weeks of boxes and furniture that doesn’t fit.

  7. The life after the problem is solved.

    A home you’re happy to invite friends to.

The question it answers

Is our customer the hero of our marketing, or are we?

When to use it

  • Rewriting a website home page.
  • Messages that talk about the company more than the customer.
  • Campaigns that need one simple story.

When to skip it

  • Detailed technical buying, where buyers need specifics more than story.

How to run it

  1. Fill each of the seven parts in one sentence.
  2. Check that the customer, not the company, is the hero.
  3. Turn the plan and call to action into the page structure.
  4. Test the page with a few target customers.

Worked example: Nordhaus Home

Nordhaus Home is a fictional chain of 35 home-goods stores.

A character
Someone moving into a new flat.
Has a problem

Ends in success

Common mistakes

  • Casting your company as the hero.
  • A plan with too many steps.
  • Overstating the stakes until it sounds like fear selling.
