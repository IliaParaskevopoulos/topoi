Content and channels

PESO Model

Four kinds of media, paid, earned, shared and owned, that work best when one piece of work feeds all of them.

Gini Dietrich, 2014

  1. Media you pay for: advertising, sponsored content, paid social and paid placements.

    Ads in GP journals and sponsored places in online medical training.

  2. Coverage others give you: press, analyst mentions, reviews, awards and speaking slots.

    A primary-care journal publishes the results of Lumora’s pilot study.

  3. What people share and discuss on social and community channels, including your own social posts.

    GPs who took part in the pilot post about it in professional forums and on LinkedIn.

  4. Channels you control: website, blog, newsletter, webinars and events.

    Lumora’s website guides, quarterly webinars and a newsletter for practice managers.

  5. The strongest programmes sit in the middle, where one piece of work feeds all four kinds of media.

    The published study becomes a webinar, gets promoted with paid ads, and is shared by the GPs who took part.

The question it answers

Which channels are we relying on, and how can one story work across all four kinds of media?

When to use it

  • Building an annual communications or content plan.
  • Checking whether you depend too much on one kind of media, often paid.
  • Planning a launch so press, social, owned channels and ads reinforce each other.

When to skip it

  • Deciding what to say. Settle positioning and messaging first.
  • Measuring results on its own. You still need metrics per channel.

How to run it

  1. List every channel you use today and sort it into paid, earned, shared or owned.
  2. Note what each one delivers and what it costs.
  3. Find the gaps: kinds of media you barely use, or depend on too heavily.
  4. Pick two or three stories strong enough to travel across all four.
  5. For each story, plan how it moves: for example, earned coverage, reused on owned channels, shared, and amplified with paid.

Worked example: Lumora Diagnostics

Lumora Diagnostics is a fictional company that makes a rapid blood-test reader for GP clinics.

Common mistakes

  • Running the four as separate teams with separate plans.
  • Counting your own social posts as earned. Earned means someone else chose to cover you.
  • Spending on paid amplification for content that has no story worth sharing.
