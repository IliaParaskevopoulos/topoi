Market and competitive analysis

TAM, SAM, SOM

Three nested market sizes: everyone who could ever buy, those you can actually serve, and the share you can realistically win.

Common market-sizing practice; no single author

  1. TAM: the total demand for this kind of product if every possible customer bought it.

    Every small business in Switzerland that invoices other businesses.

  2. SAM: the part of TAM your product and channels can actually serve today.

    Those using accounting software Kontor connects to, who wait 30 days or more to be paid.

  3. SOM: the share of SAM you can realistically win in a set period, given competition and your resources.

    What Kontor can win in three years with its current team: about 4,000 businesses.

  4. Build the numbers from customer counts and price, not from a top-down percentage of a big figure.

    4,000 businesses × CHF 1,200 in yearly fees = CHF 4.8 million, the number Kontor plans against.

The question it answers

How big is the market we can actually win, and is it worth the effort?

When to use it

  • Business plans and investor decks.
  • Deciding whether a new segment is worth entering.
  • Setting realistic revenue targets for a launch.

When to skip it

  • As a forecast on its own. SOM is a planning assumption, not a prediction.
  • When the category does not exist yet. Size the problem instead.

How to run it

  1. Define the customer precisely: who, where, with which need.
  2. Count customers and multiply by realistic yearly spend for TAM.
  3. Narrow to the customers your product and channels reach today for SAM.
  4. Estimate a win rate from your sales capacity and competition for SOM.
  5. Write down every assumption so others can challenge it.

Worked example: Kontor

Kontor is a fictional fintech that lets small businesses get paid early on their invoices.

Total addressable market
Every small business in Switzerland that invoices other businesses.
Serviceable available market
Those using accounting software Kontor connects to, who wait 30 days or more to be paid.
Serviceable obtainable market
What Kontor can win in three years with its current team: about 4,000 businesses.
Check it bottom-up
4,000 businesses × CHF 1,200 in yearly fees = CHF 4.8 million, the number Kontor plans against.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting a huge industry figure as TAM.
  • Assuming “just 1% of the market” without showing how you would win it.
  • Leaving out the assumptions, so nobody can check the numbers.
