Sales enablement

Battlecard

A one-page guide that helps sellers win against a specific competitor.

Standard product marketing practice; no single author

  1. The centre: the one-line summary of when and why you win against this competitor.

    We win when speed and flexibility matter more than the lowest rate.

  2. What the competitor says about itself, so sellers are not surprised.

    Bank factoring: “A trusted bank with the lowest rates.”

  3. Your real strengths against them, in the buyer’s terms.

    Cash in 24 hours, no paperwork, no commitment to finance every invoice.

  4. Be honest about their strengths, so sellers can respond.

    Very large volumes and an existing banking relationship.

  5. Questions that expose their weaknesses, and answers to their attacks on you.

    Ask: “How long does approval take, and must you assign all invoices?”

  6. Back each point with evidence sellers can show.

    Median payout six hours; 2,000 customers; no minimum volume.

The question it answers

What do our sellers need to know to win against this competitor?

When to use it

  • A competitor appears in many deals.
  • Sellers struggle to explain the difference.
  • After a competitor launch or price change.

When to skip it

  • Competitors you meet in only a few deals a year.

How to run it

  1. Start from win/loss interviews, not your own opinion.
  2. Keep it to one page that sellers can scan in a minute.
  3. Add proof to every claim.
  4. Review it monthly with sales and update it after every major competitor move.

Worked example: Kontor

Kontor is a fictional fintech that lets small businesses get paid early on their invoices.

Common mistakes

  • Long documents nobody reads.
  • Pretending the competitor has no strengths.
  • Cards that go out of date.
