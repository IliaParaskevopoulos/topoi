Customer understanding

Sean Ellis Test

One survey question that shows whether a product has product-market fit: how would you feel if you could no longer use it?

Sean Ellis, around 2009

  1. Ask active users: “How would you feel if you could no longer use the product?” The key answer is “very disappointed”.

    44% of Kontor’s active users say very disappointed.

  2. These users like the product but could live without it. Learn what would make it essential for them.

    36% say somewhat disappointed.

  3. These users do not depend on you. They are often outside your best-fit segment.

    20% say not disappointed, mostly very small businesses.

  4. Ellis found that products with 40% or more “very disappointed” tend to grow well. Then study who those users are and what they value most.

    Above the line. The “very disappointed” group is mostly construction subcontractors, so Kontor focuses there.

The question it answers

Do enough of our users depend on the product to show product-market fit?

When to use it

  • Before investing heavily in growth marketing.
  • Finding the segment that values the product most.
  • Tracking fit over time after major changes.

When to skip it

  • With users who have barely tried the product. Survey people who used it recently and more than once.

How to run it

  1. Survey active users who used the product at least twice recently.
  2. Ask the main question plus: what is the main benefit, and who would benefit most?
  3. Calculate the share who answer “very disappointed”.
  4. Profile that group and focus positioning and targeting on them.

Worked example: Kontor

Kontor is a fictional fintech that lets small businesses get paid early on their invoices.

Common mistakes

  • Surveying all sign-ups, including people who never really used it.
  • Treating 40% as a law rather than a benchmark.
  • Ignoring the follow-up questions, which hold the useful detail.
