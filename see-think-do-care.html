Growth and lifecycle

See, Think, Do, Care

Plan content and measurement by how much buying intent each audience has, from none yet to loyal customer.

Avinash Kaushik, 2013

  1. Four groups by intent. Kaushik describes them as clusters of intent rather than a strict funnel; the shape here shows how audience size shrinks.

    Orto plans its content for the year around the four groups.

  2. The largest audience that could ever be interested, with no buying intent yet. Earn attention with useful or entertaining content.

    Everyone who cooks dinner on weekdays: short recipe videos and kitchen tips.

  3. People with some intent who are starting to consider options. Help them compare and decide.

    People searching for healthy, quick dinners: nutrition comparisons and reviews.

  4. People ready to buy now. Make buying easy and give a reason to choose you.

    Shoppers in the chilled aisle: in-store offers and retailer app coupons.

  5. Existing customers who have bought more than once. Keep them, and turn them into advocates.

    Regular buyers: first taste of new flavours and recipes to go with each meal.

The question it answers

Are we speaking to people at every level of intent, and measuring each in the right way?

When to use it

  • Planning content for the year across channels.
  • Checking that all your content is not aimed only at buyers ready to purchase.
  • Choosing a metric per piece of content, instead of judging all by sales.

When to skip it

  • Very short B2B sales cycles with few, known buyers.
  • As a replacement for a sales pipeline model.

How to run it

  1. Define each group for your market in one sentence.
  2. Sort your existing content and channels into the four groups.
  3. Spot the gaps, often in See and Care.
  4. Choose one metric per group that fits its intent.
  5. Plan new content to fill the gaps and review quarterly.

Worked example: Orto Foods

Orto Foods is a fictional maker of chilled ready meals sold through Swiss supermarkets.

Common mistakes

  • Judging See content by direct sales.
  • Spending everything on Do, which shrinks the future audience.
  • Forgetting Care, where repeat purchases come from.
